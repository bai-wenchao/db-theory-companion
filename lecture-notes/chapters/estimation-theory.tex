\chapter{Estimation Theory}
\setcounter{section}{-1}   % sections start at 4.0 (see main.tex comment)
\label{ch:est}

\begin{objectives}
\item Recognize when a reported number is an \emph{estimator}, and state its bias and variance.
\item Build unbiased estimators by inverse-probability weighting; know why nonlinearity breaks them.
\item Invert tail bounds to set sample counts analytically, not by tuning.
\item Certify optimality with entropy-, communication-, or information-based lower bounds.
\end{objectives}

Database systems constantly answer questions with numbers they did not exactly compute: a
sketch reports a frequency, a sample reports a cardinality, simulated diffusions report an
influence score. Estimation theory says how far such a number can sit from the truth. It
builds an \emph{estimator} --- a function of the randomness the
system has chosen to expend --- and asks the classical questions in order: is it
\emph{unbiased} (correct in expectation), how large is its \emph{variance} or mean-squared
error, and could \emph{any} estimator do better \citep{estimation-theory-bg1,
estimation-theory-bg2}? Where the previous chapter bounded how far a random quantity strays
from its mean, this chapter supplies the upstream half: what the mean should estimate, and how
many samples make the estimate trustworthy --- together the standard systems promise, a
$(1\pm\varepsilon)$ estimate holding with probability $1-\delta$.

In the SIGMOD'26 corpus this is among the most-used theoretical tools: estimation theory is
tagged in 28 of the 228 tagged papers, the most frequent companion of the
\emph{error-bound} (16 papers) and \emph{probabilistic-guarantee} (13 papers) tags. The
corpus workflow is constant: read an estimator off a sketch, sample, or simulation; prove
it unbiased; bound its variance or range; convert to a confidence statement; union-bound
over all keys, nodes, or rounds; and, in the strongest papers, match with a lower bound
proving the error or space optimal. This chapter presents the ladder and four basic
exemplars first; Section~\ref{sec:est-advanced} adds sequential (anytime-valid)
confidence and two harder exemplars as extension reading.

\section{The tool: estimation theory}

The problem class is uniform. Some parameter $\theta$ of the data --- a frequency moment, a
distance, an influence score --- is inaccessible at the accuracy the system can afford, but
the system can run a randomized experiment: draw a sample, hash into a sketch, simulate a
diffusion, walk a graph. An estimator $\hat\theta$ is the function converting the outcome
into a reported number. Estimation theory climbs a four-rung ladder, each rung a clause a
paper must earn before printing a guarantee: \emph{correctness in expectation}
(unbiasedness), \emph{how fast the error shrinks} (variance, MSE), \emph{whether any
estimator shrinks it faster} (Cram\'er--Rao-style efficiency), and \emph{high-probability
accuracy surviving union bounds} (concentration). The exemplars show papers on each rung.

\subsection{The ladder}
\label{sec:est-ladder}

\begin{definition}[Estimator, bias, MSE]\label{def:est-estimator}
Let the data be drawn from a distribution $P_\theta$ indexed by a parameter $\theta \in \Theta$.
An \emph{estimator} is a statistic $\hat\theta = \hat\theta(X)$ not depending on $\theta$. Its
\emph{bias} is $\mathbb{E}_\theta[\hat\theta] - \theta$ and its \emph{mean-squared error} is
$\mathrm{MSE}_\theta(\hat\theta) = \mathbb{E}_\theta[(\hat\theta-\theta)^2]$. The estimator is
\emph{unbiased} if $\mathbb{E}_\theta[\hat\theta] = \theta$ for all $\theta$.
\end{definition}

The two error components separate exactly, and every design decision in a sketch or sampler is a
move between them:

\begin{proposition}[Bias--variance decomposition]\label{prop:est-mse}
For any estimator $\hat\theta$ with finite variance,
\[
\mathrm{MSE}_\theta(\hat\theta)
\;=\;
\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbb{E}_\theta[\hat\theta]-\theta\bigr)^2}_{\text{squared bias}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\hat\theta)}_{\text{variance}}. \eopm
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand $\mathbb{E}[(\hat\theta-\theta)^2] =
\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat\theta-\mathbb{E}\hat\theta)^2\bigr] +
(\mathbb{E}\hat\theta-\theta)^2 + 2(\mathbb{E}\hat\theta-\theta)\,
\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta-\mathbb{E}\hat\theta]$; the cross term vanishes.
\end{proof}

Unbiasedness is prized for a structural reason: it makes errors \emph{add}. Independent unbiased
pieces --- per-key counters, per-round messages, per-node visits --- combine into an unbiased
average whose variance is the average of the variances:

\begin{lemma}[Averaging independent copies]\label{lem:est-averaging}
Let $\hat\theta_1,\dots,\hat\theta_t$ be independent, each with
$\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_i] = \theta$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_i) \le \sigma^2$. Then
$\bar\theta = \frac1t\sum_{i=1}^t \hat\theta_i$ is unbiased and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\bar\theta) \;=\; \frac{1}{t^2}\sum_{i=1}^t
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_i) \;\le\; \frac{\sigma^2}{t}. \eopm
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Unbiasedness is linearity of expectation; independence zeroes the cross terms in
$\operatorname{Var}(\sum_i \hat\theta_i) = \sum_i \operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_i) +
\sum_{i\ne j}\operatorname{Cov}(\hat\theta_i,\hat\theta_j)$.
\end{proof}

\sidenote{Unbiasedness is \emph{fragile under nonlinearity}: $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] \ne
g(\mathbb{E}X)$, so an unbiased estimate of $x$ does not yield one of $x^p$ by plugging
in. Corpus papers either estimate the nonlinear functional directly
(Section~\ref{sec:est-bd}) or \emph{truncate}, capping the estimator and booking the
bias into the guarantee (Section~\ref{sec:est-fp}).}

The second rung asks whether the variance is not just bounded but \emph{good}. The classical
benchmark is the Cram\'er--Rao inequality: no unbiased estimator can beat the inverse of the
information the data carries about the parameter
\citep{estimation-theory-bg1,estimation-theory-bg2}.

\begin{theorem}[Cram\'er--Rao lower bound]\label{thm:est-cr}
Let $\{p_\theta\}_{\theta\in\Theta}$ be a regular parametric family (support independent of
$\theta$; differentiation and expectation exchange) and $\hat\theta$ an unbiased estimator with
finite variance. Then
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\hat\theta) \;\ge\; \frac{1}{I(\theta)},
\qquad
I(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_\theta\Bigl[\bigl(\partial_\theta \log p_\theta(X)\bigr)^2\Bigr],
\]
the \emph{Fisher information}. An unbiased estimator attaining the bound is called
\emph{efficient}. \eop
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
The \emph{score} $S = \partial_\theta\log p_\theta(X)$ has mean zero and covariance $1$ with
any unbiased estimator; Cauchy--Schwarz gives $1 \le \operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)\cdot
\operatorname{Var}(S) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)\,I(\theta)$ (full proof in
Section~\ref{sec:est-appendix}).
\end{proof}

\sidenote{In database papers the ``information'' is rarely Fisherian --- the data model is
combinatorial, not a smooth family. The Cram\'er--Rao \emph{role} --- no estimator can do
better --- is played by entropy-incompressibility (Section~\ref{sec:est-sublime}),
communication reductions (Section~\ref{sec:est-fp}), and minimax arguments: whatever the
estimator must distinguish dictates the samples or bits it must pay.}

The third and fourth rungs convert moments into the contracts systems actually print. First the
cheap, moment-only rung:

\begin{proposition}[Variance to confidence: the Chebyshev rung]\label{prop:est-chebyshev}
Let $\hat\theta$ be unbiased with $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta) \le \sigma^2$, and let $\bar\theta$
average $t$ independent copies. Then for any accuracy $E > 0$ and failure probability $\delta > 0$,
choosing $t \ge \sigma^2/(\delta E^2)$ gives
\[
\Pr\bigl[\,|\bar\theta - \theta| \ge E\,\bigr] \;\le\; \delta. \eopm
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Chebyshev: $\Pr[|\bar\theta-\theta| \ge E] \le \operatorname{Var}(\bar\theta)/E^2 \le
\sigma^2/(tE^2) \le \delta$.
\end{proof}

Chebyshev spends samples proportionally to $1/\delta$ --- fine for one estimate, unaffordable over
many. When the copies are bounded, the concentration rung spends only logarithmically:

\begin{proposition}[Sample-count inversion: the Hoeffding rung]\label{prop:est-hoeffding-inv}
Let $\hat\theta_1,\dots,\hat\theta_t$ be i.i.d.\ copies of an unbiased estimator with
$\hat\theta_i \in [a, a+r]$ a.s.\ (range width $r$), and $\bar\theta$ their average. Then
\[
\Pr\bigl[\,|\bar\theta - \theta| \ge E\,\bigr] \;\le\; 2\exp\!\Bigl(-\,\frac{2tE^2}{r^2}\Bigr),
\]
so $t = r^2\ln(2M/\delta)/(2E^2)$ makes $M$ such estimates simultaneously $(E,\delta)$-accurate.
\eop
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Additive Hoeffding on bounded i.i.d.\ summands
\citep{estimation-theory-bg3,estimation-theory-bg4}, then a union bound over the $M$ estimates:
each fails with probability at most $\delta/M$.
\end{proof}

This inversion is the most reusable formula of the chapter: samples $=$ range squared $\times$
$\log(\text{events}/\delta)$ over twice the squared accuracy --- derived, never tuned.
Sections~\ref{sec:est-bd} and~\ref{sec:est-tcim} run exactly this computation, with
$r = d_v\Delta$ and $M = n$ nodes.

\begin{remarkbox}[Notation]
The generic parameter of \S\ref{sec:est-ladder} is $\theta$ and its estimator $\hat\theta$. An
\emph{$(E,\delta)$-estimate} means $|\hat\theta - \theta| \le E$ with probability at least
$1-\delta$; a \emph{$(1\pm\varepsilon)$ estimate} is the multiplicative form. Sample counts
appear as $t$ (generic), $\omega_t$ (trees, Section~\ref{sec:est-bd}), and $\theta$ again in
$\theta_{\max}$ (sample-set threshold, Section~\ref{sec:est-tcim}) --- always redefined
locally. Space upper bounds are stated in machine \emph{words}, lower bounds in \emph{bits}.
\end{remarkbox}

\subsection{How to choose an estimator}
\label{sec:est-choose}

The selection procedure is mechanical once three questions are answered. \emph{First, what
is the parameter, and what is the randomness?} Name the quantity $\theta$ and the random
experiment that touches it; the estimator is whatever function of the outcome you report.
\emph{Second, can the expectation be computed exactly?} If each sampled object carries a
known inclusion probability, weight by its inverse (Horvitz--Thompson): the weighted sum
is unbiased by linearity alone; a nonlinear functional is estimated directly per copy,
not by plugging into a good estimate of something else. \emph{Third, what do you control
--- the variance or the range?} Variance only: the Chebyshev rung
(Proposition~\ref{prop:est-chebyshev}), $t \propto 1/(\delta E^2)$, boostable by
medians. Bounded range: the Hoeffding rung (Proposition~\ref{prop:est-hoeffding-inv}),
$t = r^2\ln(2M/\delta)/(2E^2)$ --- derive the count, do not tune it. If neither is tame,
cap the estimator and pay the bias into the guarantee, as Section~\ref{sec:est-fp} does.

\begin{reachbox}{When to Reach for Estimation Theory}
Reach whenever a system reports numbers from a sample, sketch, simulation, or randomized
mechanism --- approximate query processing, cardinality and frequency estimation, graph
distances, influence and pattern counts --- and the deliverable is an error bar or an
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$ contract; or when a lower bound must certify the design optimal. Recipe:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Name the parameter $\theta$ and the random experiment; write the reported number as a
  function $\hat\theta$ of the outcome.
\item Make $\hat\theta$ unbiased by inverse-probability weighting (indicator $\times$
  $1/\Pr[\text{inclusion}]$); verify $\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta] = \theta$ by linearity.
\item If heavy tails blow up the variance, cap or shrink the estimator and book the induced
  bias into the approximation factor (the ledger of Proposition~\ref{prop:est-mse}).
\item Bound what you control --- $\operatorname{Var}$ (Chebyshev rung, $t \propto
  \sigma^2/(\delta E^2)$) or range width $r$ (Hoeffding rung, $t =
  r^2\ln(2M/\delta)/(2E^2)$) --- and invert for the sample count analytically.
\item Union-bound over every key, node, or coordinate that must be simultaneously correct;
  the $\ln(2M/\delta)$ in the count is that union bound, pre-paid.
\item Certify optimality (entropy incompressibility, a communication reduction, or
  Theorem~\ref{thm:est-cr}); matching within logarithmic factors is the gold standard.
\end{enumerate}
\end{reachbox}

\section{Frequency moments from noisy, ambiguous streams}
\label{sec:est-fp}

The exemplar is a PODS'26 paper on streaming statistics under noisy identity resolution
\citep{sigmod26-115}\pods. Frequency moments, $F_p = \sum_j f_j^p$, are canonical streaming
quantities behind cardinality, skew, and heavy-hitter analytics, and decades of sketching theory
estimate them accurately when every item is identified exactly. Real pipelines resolve
identities imperfectly: each item arrives \emph{ambiguous}, compatible with several
ground-truth identities, as after record matching or noisy deduplication. The paper
introduces a per-instance noise measure, the $F_p$-\emph{mismatch-ambiguity} $\Lambda_p$,
and proves that $F_p$ of the unknown ground
truth is approximable to factor $(\varepsilon + O(\Lambda_p))$ in one pass over $m$ items with
$O(\varepsilon^{-2} m^{1-1/p})$ words of space, essentially optimally. The algorithm is
estimation theory in one exhibit: an unbiased ordered-clique sampler whose variance is tamed by
truncation, with H\"older supplying the moment bound and Chebyshev the confidence.

\paragraph{Problem.}
A noisy dataset is a stream $\sigma = (\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_m)$ of items over a universe $U$;
the ground truth is an assignment of each position to a true identity, $\mu = (\mu_1,\dots,
\mu_m) \in U^m$. Writing $f_j = |\{i : \mu_i = u_j\}|$, the goal is $F_p(\mu) = \sum_{j=1}^n
f_j^p$ for constant $p \ge 1$, but the algorithm sees only $\sigma$. Define two positions as
\emph{similar} ($\sigma_i \sim \sigma_{i'}$) when they are compatible with a common identity, and
let $G^\sigma = ([m], E^\sigma)$ be the resulting \emph{similarity graph}; write $B_i^\sigma$ for
the clique of position $i$ in $G^\sigma$ and $B_i^\mu$ for its ground-truth clique (the positions
sharing $i$'s true identity). The paper's per-instance noise measure is:

\begin{definition}[$F_p$-mismatch-ambiguity; Definition of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{def:est-fp-lambda}
For $p \ge 1$,
\[
\Lambda_p(\sigma,\mu) \;=\; \frac{1}{F_p(\mu)} \sum_{i\in[m]}
\Bigl(\bigl|B_i^\sigma \cup B_i^\mu\bigr|^{p-1} -
\bigl|B_i^\sigma \cap B_i^\mu\bigr|^{p-1}\Bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

When $\sigma = \mu$ the cliques coincide and $\Lambda_p = 0$; the quantity measures the
relative excess clique mass the ambiguity injects. A $(c, 0.01)$-approximation means an
estimate within factor $(1\pm c)$ of $F_p(\mu)$ with probability at least $0.99$.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[One-pass estimation under mismatch ambiguity; Theorem of the paper, compressed]
\label{thm:est-fp-upper}
For any constant $p \ge 1$ and any noisy stream of length $m$ with
$\Lambda_p \le 1/(3\cdot p!)$, there is a one-pass algorithm computing an
$\bigl(\varepsilon + O(\Lambda_p),\, 0.01\bigr)$-approximation of $F_p(\mu)$ using
$O(\varepsilon^{-2} m^{1-1/p})$ words of space. \eop
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Space lower bound; Corollary of the paper, compressed]\label{thm:est-fp-lower}
For any constants $p > 1$, $C > 0$ and $\varepsilon \in (0,\tfrac13)$, any $O(1)$-pass
$\bigl(\varepsilon + C\Lambda_p,\, 0.48\bigr)$-approximation algorithm for $F_p$ over noisy
streams of length at most $m$ requires $\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/p} m^{1-1/p})$ bits of space.
\eop
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How estimation theory is applied.}
The random object is an \emph{ordered $p$-clique} of the observable similarity graph --- a tuple
$(i_1,\dots,i_p)$ of positions that are pairwise similar. The key identity is that clique mass
\emph{is} the frequency moment: for noiseless data the ground-truth cliques partition $[m]$ into
classes $V_1,\dots,V_{n_\mu}$ of sizes equal to the frequencies, so the ordered-clique count is
$\sum_k |V_k|^p = F_p(\mu)$ (Fact of the paper); a divergence lemma bounds the noisy count,
$|K_p^\sigma \setminus K_p^\mu| \le \Lambda_p F_p$ and $|K_p^\mu \setminus K_p^\sigma| \le
\tfrac{p}{2}\Lambda_p F_p$ (Lemma of the paper), so the observable clique mass is a
$(1 + O(\Lambda_p))$-proxy for the target. The estimator is pure Horvitz--Thompson: sample
$I_1$ uniformly from $[m]$ and, given the prefix, $I_k$ uniformly from the \emph{tail set}
$\Phi(I_1,\dots,I_{k-1}) = \{i \ge I_{k-1} : i \sim I_{j'} \text{ for all } j' < k\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Unbiased clique-count estimator; Lemma of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{lem:est-fp-unbiased}
Let $R_1 = m$ and $R_k = |\Phi(I_1,\dots,I_{k-1})|$ for $k = 2,\dots,p$, and let
$X = \prod_{k\in[p]} R_k$. Then
\[
\mathbb{E}[X] \;=\; \bigl|K_p^\sigma\bigr|, \eopm
\]
the number of increasingly ordered $p$-cliques of $G^\sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each fixed increasingly ordered clique is sampled with probability exactly $\frac1m
\prod_{k=2}^p \frac{1}{|\Phi(i_1,\dots,i_{k-1})|}$ and then contributes $X = \prod_k R_k$,
the reciprocal of that probability; summing over cliques (linearity) counts each once.
\end{proof}

The raw estimator is unbiased but heavy-tailed --- a draw into a huge clique inflates $X$ ---
so the paper truncates: $Y = \min\{X,\; m\,(d_I^\mu)^{p-1}\}$ with $d_I^\mu$ the ground-truth
clique size of the sampled position $I$. The truncation level makes the bias land inside the
$\Lambda_p$ term of the guarantee while the second moment collapses to a H\"older consequence:

\begin{lemma}[Truncation controls the variance; Claim of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{lem:est-fp-trunc}
With $Y$ as above, $(1-2\Lambda_p)F_p(\mu) \le \mathbb{E}[Y] \le F_p(\mu)$ and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y) \;\le\; \mathbb{E}[Y^2] \;\le\; m^{1-1/p}\, F_p(\mu)^2. \eopm
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The sandwich sums per-position clipping errors into $\Lambda_p F_p$; the moment bound chains
$Y \le m \max_k |V_k|^{p-1}$ with $\max_k |V_k|^{p-1} \le F_p^{1-1/p}$ and H\"older's $m =
\sum_k |V_k| \le n_\mu^{1-1/p} F_p^{1/p}$ (full derivation in
Section~\ref{sec:est-appendix}).
\end{proof}

The confidence assembly runs on the ladder's rungs. \emph{Lower tail}: Chebyshev
(Proposition~\ref{prop:est-chebyshev}) on $t = \Theta(\varepsilon^{-2}m^{1-1/p})$ copies gives
$\Pr[\bar Y < (1-\varepsilon-2\Lambda_p)F_p] \le 10^{-5}$. \emph{Upper tail}: a R\'enyi-style
inequality due to Feige --- for independent nonnegative $Z_i$ with means $\mu_i \le 1$,
$\Pr[\sum_i Z_i < \sum_i \mu_i + \delta] > \min(\frac{\delta}{1+\delta},\frac1{13})$ --- shows
the average is not too \emph{large} with constant probability, boosted to $0.99$ by
repetition. The space bound is this $t$, and the lower bound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:est-fp-lower}) is the estimation-theoretic mirror: DISJ reduces to
$F_p$-under-ambiguity --- each player's bits become items whose similarity pattern encodes
set intersection --- so beating the stated space would solve DISJ with too little
communication.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
The streaming engine reports frequency-moment analytics over dirty, unresolved data with a
certificate that degrades gracefully with the ambiguity: error $\varepsilon + O(\Lambda_p)$,
one pass, sublinear space, and a lower bound proving nothing substantially better exists. The
pattern --- unbiased Horvitz--Thompson core, truncation paid into the approximation factor,
Chebyshev below and a reverse-Markov bound above --- transfers to any heavy-tailed estimator.

\section{Epoch analysis and an entropy bound for expandable sketches}
\label{sec:est-sublime}

The exemplar is a SIGMOD'26 paper on sketches for unbounded streams \citep{sigmod26-231}.
Frequency-estimation (FE) sketches --- Count-Min, CountSketch --- are standard equipment, but they
commit to a fixed width at initialization, while real streams have unknown, unbounded length and
heavy skew: a fixed sketch either wastes memory up front or degrades silently as it overflows.
The paper's system, \emph{Sublime}, is an expandable, CountSketch-style FE sketch that grows
with the stream while keeping every per-key estimate unbiased, supported by a two-sided theory:
a variance recursion over the expansion \emph{epochs} shows the estimation error stays sublinear
in the stream length, a matching memory analysis bounds the footprint of the skewed counters,
and an entropy-incompressibility argument proves any expandable FE sketch must pay within a
small logarithmic factor of Sublime's space.

\paragraph{Problem.}
A stream of keys $y$ (insertions and deletions) is processed by a sketch answering frequency
queries $f(y)$ at any time; the total length $N$ is unknown and unbounded, and the frequency
distribution is skewed, so counters holding heavy keys must encode large values compactly.
\emph{Sublime} maintains $d$ counter arrays whose width follows a \emph{size function}
$W(\cdot)$ --- e.g.\ $W(N) = N/\varepsilon$ (linear) or $W(N) = N^\alpha/\varepsilon$
(sublinear, $0 \le \alpha < 1$) --- and expands over \emph{epochs}: at epoch $j$ the width is
$W_j = W(N_j)$, $N_j$ the keys seen by epoch $j$'s start. Expansion rehashes and
\emph{decrements} counters rather than rebuilding, so old contributions stay visible; the price
is that updates from epoch $j$ survive into epoch $i$'s counters only with probability
$2^{i-j}/W_i$. The target is the per-key frequency $f(y)$, estimated CountSketch-style by
signed counter reads.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{lemma}[Variance recursion over epochs; Lemma of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{lem:est-sublime-var}
The estimates are unbiased, like CountSketch's. Moreover, letting $V(F)$ denote the estimation
variance under size function $W(\cdot)$ and one array, on a stream with squared $\ell_2$-norm
$F = \sum_y (f(y))^2$, at the end of epoch $i$:
\[
V(F_{i+1}) \;\le\; \frac{1}{W_i}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{i} 2^{\,i-j}\cdot\bigl(F_{j+1} - F_j\bigr),
\]
where $F_j$ is the squared $\ell_2$-norm at the start of epoch $j$. \eop
\end{lemma}

The recursion telescopes (geometric series in $2^{\,i-j}$, evaluated per size function): for
$W(N) = N^\alpha/\varepsilon$ the error is $O(N/W(N))$ --- sublinear --- while for $W(N) =
N/\varepsilon$ it is $O(\varepsilon\log N)$. The complementary memory statement is:

\begin{theorem}[Space lower bound for expandable FE sketches; Theorem of the paper]
\label{thm:est-sublime-lb}
Consider an FE sketch with only overestimation errors that processes growing streams with an
error bound of $E(N)$ and a confidence of $1-\delta$. Such a sketch must use at least
\[
\frac{N}{E(N)}\cdot\Bigl[\Omega\Bigl(\log\tfrac{1}{\delta} +
\log\log\tfrac{N}{E(N)}\Bigr) - \Theta(1)\Bigr] \eopm
\]
bits of memory at some point in time while processing a stream.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How estimation theory is applied.}
The random objects are the hash placements and the $\pm1$ signs of the CountSketch read;
unbiasedness is the classical sign-orthogonality computation ($\mathbb{E}[\sigma(x)\sigma(y)] = 0$
for $x \ne y$), re-run with the epoch structure: a key's contribution from epoch $j$ reaches the
current counter of a query only with probability $2^{i-j}/W_i$, so the variance integrates those
geometric weights over the $\ell_2$-mass \emph{increments} $(F_{j+1}-F_j)$ --- the variance term
of Proposition~\ref{prop:est-mse}, bookkept per epoch. Confidence is the Chebyshev rung
(Proposition~\ref{prop:est-chebyshev}): a sketch with $|\mathbb{E}[\hat f(x)] - f(x)| \le E(N)$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(\hat f(x)) \le \delta E(N)^2$ guarantees error $2E(N)$ at confidence
$1-\delta$ by Chebyshev, and the theorem holds for any such sketch. The memory \emph{upper}
bound is where convexity earns its keep: counters are encoded as short \emph{stubs} plus rare
\emph{extensions}, and since the logarithm is concave, Jensen bounds the total stub length by
the logarithm of the \emph{average} counter value --- $w$ counters with total absolute value
$k$ encode in $w\cdot[4.41 + 1.26\log_2(k/w)]$ bits (Lemma of the paper). The lower bound is
the entropy-incompressibility mirror in the Cram\'er--Rao role: a smaller sketch would compress
sequences of $N/E(N)$ keys from a large universe below their entropy --- using the law of total
variance to find an epoch state with small second moment, then fixing the internal randomness
so the state plus a few bits reconstruct the sequence. Information cannot be destroyed.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
The system gets a frequency-estimation sketch whose accuracy and memory are certified
\emph{while it grows}, with no prior knowledge of the stream's length or skew: near-sublinear
error at confidence $1-\delta$, a footprint of roughly $d\cdot W(N)\cdot[4.41 +
1.26\log_2(N/W(N))]$ bits, and a lower bound within a small log factor of optimal.

\section{Spanning-tree estimators for biharmonic distance}
\label{sec:est-bd}

The exemplar is a SIGMOD'26 paper on graph distance estimation \citep{sigmod26-102}. The
\emph{biharmonic distance} (BD) between two nodes is defined through the square of the
Laplacian pseudoinverse; like resistance distance it rewards \emph{multiple} connecting
paths, and it is more robust to noise and degree skew than shortest-path or resistance
measures --- attractive for similarity search and network analysis. But its definition
seems to demand global matrix computations: exact BD needs the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse
($O(n^3)$), and the fastest prior approximations need $\widetilde{O}(m/\varepsilon^2)$
preprocessing over the whole graph --- prohibitive when only a few pairs matter. The
paper instead answers BD queries for a \emph{queried set} of pairs locally: reformulate
BD around a pivot via Sherman--Morrison, interpret each term as a statistic of absorbing
random walks, and estimate those statistics by averaging sampled spanning trees --- the
tree count \emph{derived} from the Hoeffding inversion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:est-hoeffding-inv}, pivot degree times diameter as the range.

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let $G = (V,E)$ be a connected undirected graph with Laplacian $L$ and pseudoinverse
$L^\dagger$. For distinct $s,t \in V$ and $\mathbf{b}_{st} = \chi_s - \chi_t$, the biharmonic
distance is $\beta(s,t) := \mathbf{b}_{st}^\top (L^\dagger)^2\, \mathbf{b}_{st}$
(Definition~2.1 of the paper, notation cleaned). Fix a \emph{pivot} $v \in V$ and let $L_v$ be
$L$ with row and column $v$ deleted, and $a$ the column of $L$ linking $v$ to the rest. The
paper's reformulation --- Sherman--Morrison applied to the $v$-deleted Laplacian square
$L_v^2 + aa^\top$ (Lemma~3.1 of the paper) --- reduces BD to quantities of $L_v^{-1}$:

\begin{theorem}[Pivoted reformulation of BD; Theorem~3.5 of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{thm:est-bd-reform}
For any pivot $v$ and $s,t \ne v$,
\[
\beta(s,t) \;=\; \mathbf{x}_b^\top \mathbf{x}_b -
\frac{(\mathbf{x}_b^\top \mathbf{x}_a)^2}{1 + \mathbf{x}_a^\top \mathbf{x}_a},
\qquad
\mathbf{x}_a = a^\top L_v^{-1},\;\; \mathbf{x}_b = \mathbf{b}_{st}^\top L_v^{-1},
\]
i.e., BD is a rational function of the two vectors $\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{x}_b \in
\mathbb{R}^{V\setminus\{v\}}$. \eop
\end{theorem}

So the problem becomes: estimate the $n$ coordinates of $\mathbf{x}_a$ and $\mathbf{x}_b$ well
enough that the rational function is accurate. Two structural facts make the coordinates
estimable without inverting anything. First, $(L_v^{-1})_{us} = \pi^v(u,s)/d_s$, where
$\pi^v(u,s)$ is the expected number of visits to $s$ of a random walk from $u$ absorbed at
$v$ --- the \emph{absorbing-random-walk interpretation} of the inverse. Second, the paper's
\emph{BackPush} computes the needed visit counts locally via the invariant

\begin{lemma}[Pushback invariant; Lemma~4.1 of the paper]\label{lem:est-bd-invariant}
For each node $u \in V \setminus \{v\}$ and target $x$, throughout the algorithm,
\[
\pi^v(u,x) \;=\; \hat\pi^v(u,x) + \sum_{w \in V} r(w,x)\,\pi^v(u,w), \eopm
\]
where $\hat\pi^v(u,x)$ is the (partial) estimate returned so far and $r(w,x)$ the pending
residual at $w$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction over pushback operations: initially all mass is residual; pushing back at node $y$ moves
$r(y,x)/d_y$ to each neighbor's residual (and to $\hat\pi$ when $y = u$), and the walk
interpretation of $\pi^v(\cdot,x)$ makes the two sides change by exactly the same amount.
\end{proof}

The invariant certifies that when residuals are small the local estimate is exact
(additive error $\sum_w \pi^v(u,w)\, r^{\max}$, Lemma~4.2 of the paper) --- but for estimates, the
paper's \emph{FastTree} replaces deterministic pushes by sampling.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[Tree-sampling accuracy; Theorem~6.3 of the paper]\label{thm:est-bd-fasttree}
For error parameter $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ and failure probability $\delta \in (0,1)$, let
\[
\omega_t \;=\; \frac{d_v^2\,\Delta^2 \log(2n/\delta)}{2\varepsilon^2},
\]
where $d_v$ is the pivot degree and $\Delta$ the graph diameter. Then sampling $\omega_t$ spanning
trees yields estimators $\tilde x_a(u), \tilde x_b(u)$ satisfying, for every node $u$,
\[
\bigl|x_a(u) - \tilde x_a(u)\bigr| \le \varepsilon
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bigl|x_b(u) - \tilde x_b(u)\bigr| \le \varepsilon, \eopm
\]
and FastTree runs in $O\bigl(\omega_t \cdot (h_{s,v} + h_{t,v} + \sum_{w\in N(v)} h_{w,v})\bigr)$
time, where $h_{u,v}$ is the hitting time from $u$ to $v$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How estimation theory is applied.}
The random object is a uniform spanning tree.\footnote{Sampled by Wilson's algorithm, which runs a
loop-erased random walk to the root; its cost is governed by the same hitting times that appear in
the running-time bound.} The bridge from trees to Laplacian entries is an electrical-network
identity the paper proves with Kirchhoff's laws:

\begin{lemma}[Spanning-tree expression of the inverse; Lemma~6.1 of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{lem:est-bd-kirchhoff}
Let $N$ be the number of spanning trees of $G$, let $N_{sv}(a,b)$ be the number of spanning trees
in which the unique path from $s$ to $v$ traverses the edge $(a,b)$ in this order, and let
$P_{uv}$ be any path from $u$ to $v$. Then
\[
\bigl(L_v^{-1}\bigr)_{su} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{(a,b) \in P_{uv}}
\bigl(N_{sv}(a,b) - N_{sv}(b,a)\bigr). \eopm
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Treat $G$ as a unit-resistor network with unit current injected at $s$ and extracted at $v$: the
current on edge $(a,b)$ is exactly $\bigl(N_{sv}(a,b)-N_{sv}(b,a)\bigr)/N$ (a classical
Kirchhoff fact), the voltage at $u$ is the current summed along any path $P_{uv}$, and
$(L_v^{-1})_{su}$ \emph{is} that voltage.
\end{proof}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:est-bd-kirchhoff}, each coordinate $x_a(u)$, $x_b(u)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:est-bd-reform} is a sum of oriented edge indicators along paths of a random
tree, so a single tree yields per-coordinate contributions whose expectations are the true
coordinates, and averaging $\omega_t$ independent trees gives unbiased estimators
$\tilde x_a(u), \tilde x_b(u)$ (Corollary~6.2 of the paper; its walk-based sibling averages
the collision estimators of Lemma~5.1). The variance side is the Hoeffding rung in its purest
form: each per-tree contribution is bounded, $|\tilde x_a^i(u)| \le d_v\Delta$ and
$|\tilde x_b^i(u)| \le 2\Delta$ (range bounds of Corollary~6.2, at most $d_v$ neighbor paths
feeding $\mathbf{x}_a$). Additive Hoeffding then gives per-node failure $\delta/n$ at exactly
the displayed $\omega_t$, and the union bound over the $n$ nodes --- pre-paid as the
$\log(2n/\delta)$ inside $\omega_t$ --- delivers the simultaneous statement (proof in
Section~\ref{sec:est-appendix}); the tuning is mechanical, $r = d_v\Delta$, $M = 2n$.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
The graph engine answers biharmonic-distance queries for targeted node pairs in time governed
by hitting times between the query nodes and the pivot --- no $O(n^3)$ pseudoinverse, no
whole-graph preprocessing. Every coordinate carries its accuracy certificate, and the sample
count was derived, not tuned --- the chapter's formula applied verbatim.

\section{Concentration-sized sampling for influence blocking}
\label{sec:est-tcim}

The exemplar is a SIGMOD'26 paper on curbing misinformation spread \citep{sigmod26-373}. When
false information diffuses through a social network, harm depends on \emph{time}: a node
infected early contributes more damage, so interventions have a deadline. The paper studies
\emph{time-critical influence minimization} (TCIM): given a diffusion process with edge
probabilities and delays, a seed set of misinformation, a deadline $T$, and a budget $k$, choose
$k$ nodes to block so that the total \emph{activation-duration-weighted} spread is minimized.
The objective is maximally hostile to exact optimization: evaluating the blocking benefit
$D(B)$ is \#P-hard, $D$ is monotone but \emph{non-submodular}, and no polynomial-time method
can approximate TCIM within any constant factor unless $P = NP$. The algorithm, CBFM,
therefore \emph{sandwiches} the objective between submodular surrogates $D_L \le D \le D_U$,
maximizes them greedily on randomly sampled \emph{reverse-score sample sets}, and --- the
estimation-theoretic core --- derives unbiased coverage estimators and concentration bounds
that set the number of sample sets analytically and power anytime stopping, with the final
guarantee holding with probability $1-3\delta$.

\paragraph{Problem.}
Diffusion follows the independent-cascade model with delays: each edge $\langle u,v\rangle$ is
live with probability $p(u,v)$ and carries a random delay, inducing a distribution over
\emph{possible worlds} $w$; node $v$'s activation time $t_v^w$ determines its weight
$U(T - t_v^w)$ under a monotone weight function $U$, zero past the deadline $T$. The
\emph{AD influence spread} is $\sigma_G^T(S) = \sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}[U(T-t_v)]$, and the
blocking benefit of $B \subseteq V\setminus S$ is $D(B) = \sigma_G^T(S) -
\sigma_G^T[V\setminus B](S)$; the goal is a size-$k$ $B$ maximizing $D(B)$. Since $D$ resists
optimization, CBFM maximizes the submodular bounds $D_L \le D \le D_U$ ($D_L$ from delayed
activations, $D_U$ from \emph{time-aware reachable trees}; Lemmas 4.1--4.8 of the paper),
where evaluating either bound is again \#P-hard (Lemma~5.1 of the paper) --- hence estimation.
A \emph{GRS/TRS sample set} $R_i$ draws a source node with probability proportional to a
computable weight and records, in a random possible world, which candidate blockers would
\emph{reverse-reach} the source before $T$, each with its potential score; $X_i(B)$ is the
total score in $R_i$ covered by $B$, and $\Lambda_R(B) = \sum_{i=1}^{\theta} X_i(B)$ the
\emph{coverage} of $B$ over $\theta$ sample sets.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[Approximation guarantee of CBFM; Theorem~6.7 of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{thm:est-tcim-main}
Given $0 \le \varepsilon,\delta \le 1$, CBFM returns $B^\ast$ satisfying
\[
\Pr\Bigl[D\bigl(B^\ast\bigr) \;\ge\; \tau \bigl(1 - \tfrac{1}{e} - \varepsilon\bigr)\cdot
D(B_o)\Bigr] \;\ge\; 1 - 3\delta, \eopm
\]
where $B_o$ is the optimal size-$k$ blocker set and
$\tau = \max\bigl\{D_L(B_o^L)/D(B_o),\; D_U(B_U^o)\cdot(1-\varepsilon)/\bigl((1+\varepsilon)
D(B_o)\bigr)\bigr\}$ is a data-driven sandwich ratio.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How estimation theory is applied.}
The random objects are the reverse-score sample sets; the estimated parameters are the surrogate
objectives $\psi \in \{D_L, D_U\}$. The workhorse is an unbiasedness lemma of exactly the
Horvitz--Thompson shape of the ladder:

\begin{lemma}[Unbiased coverage estimation; Lemma~5.8 of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{lem:est-tcim-unbiased}
For any $B \subseteq V\setminus S$ and generalized sample set $R_i$,
\[
\psi(B) \;=\; I_{T^\ast}(S)\cdot \mathbb{E}\bigl[X_i(B)\bigr], \eopm
\]
the expectation over the randomness of $R_i$, where $I_{T^\ast}(S)$ is the computable total
reverse-reachability weight --- the normalizer of the weighted source sampling. Consequently,
with $\theta$ sample sets, $I_{T^\ast}(S)\cdot \Lambda_R(B)/\theta$ is an unbiased estimator of
$\psi(B)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each possible world enters the sample with probability proportional to its weight; the weighted
source choice times the recorded score is the world's contribution to $\psi(B)$ divided by its
inclusion probability, so expectation sums the worlds back in (the RIS accounting of
influence-maximization sampling, re-derived for time-aware scores).
\end{proof}

The second ingredient converts the estimator into a \emph{stopping rule}: concentration of the
coverage around its mean, with the target value itself as the variance proxy.

\begin{lemma}[Coverage concentration; Lemma~6.4 of the paper, compressed]\label{lem:est-tcim-conc}
For any $\lambda > 0$, the coverage deviation obeys a Bernstein-style two-tail bound:
\[
\Pr\Bigl[\bigl|\Lambda_R(B)/\theta - \psi(B)\bigr| \;\ge\; \lambda\cdot U(T-1)\Bigr] \;\le\;
2\exp\!\Bigl(-\,\Theta\Bigl(\frac{\theta\,\psi(B)\,\lambda^2}
{I_{T^\ast}(S)\,U(T-1)}\Bigr)\Bigr),
\]
i.e.\ deviations decay exponentially in $\theta\cdot\psi(B)$, at range scale
$I_{T^\ast}(S)\,U(T-1)$. \eop
\end{lemma}

Inverting this bound --- the same move as Proposition~\ref{prop:est-hoeffding-inv}, variance
proxy in place of squared range --- yields the threshold analytically (Lemma~6.5 of the paper,
compressed): $\theta_{\max} = \widetilde{O}\bigl(n\,U(T-1)/(\varepsilon^2\cdot
\mathrm{OPT}_L)\bigr)$ with $\mathrm{OPT}_L$ a computable lower bound on the optimum ($U(1)$
times the $k$ largest activation probabilities). The threshold certifies greedy on
$\theta_{\max}$ sample sets $(1-\tfrac1e-\varepsilon)$-approximate for both surrogates with
failure $\delta/3$ each; a third $\delta/3$ guards the anytime stopping rule, which
estimates the sandwich ratio $\tau$ on the fly and stops once it cannot improve.
A union bound over the three events assembles Theorem~\ref{thm:est-tcim-main} --- the $3\delta$,
itemized.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
The system selects blockers on graphs with billions of edges with a \emph{certified},
instance-adapted guarantee (the ratio $\tau$ measures how tightly the submodular sandwich hugs
$D$), while the concentration-derived threshold and stopping rule mean it draws exactly as
many sample sets as the accuracy target demands --- the modern template for
influence-style optimization under simulation access.

\section{Advanced usage and further reading}
\label{sec:est-advanced}

Everything so far fixes the sample size in advance; the two papers sketched here instead let
the \emph{data} decide when to stop, or rest unbiasedness on a sampling invariant.

\paragraph{Betting confidence sequences as stopping rules \citep{sigmod26-293}.}
When an expensive oracle --- an LLM, a human --- processes records behind a cheap proxy
score, the system routes records above a cascade threshold $\theta$ to the oracle and
must certify that recall meets a target $T$ with few oracle calls. Checking a fixed-sample
confidence interval after every record invalidates its $\delta$: errors stack over
repeated looks. The paper adopts \emph{anytime-valid} confidence sequences derived from
betting (empirical-Bernstein capital processes of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, simplified):
the capital $K(m,Y) = \prod_j \bigl(1 + \min(\lambda_j, \tfrac{3}{4m})(Y_j - m)\bigr)$,
bets tuned to the running variance, stays bounded in expectation unless the mean crosses
$m$, so a threshold on $K$ certifies a mean bound at \emph{every} sample count
simultaneously. Estimation becomes a \emph{sequential stopping rule}: sample, update the
capital, stop when the certified recall lower bound clears the target; a
without-replacement variant handles finite populations, and a companion lower bound
prices any proxy-monotone sampler by its precision cost.

\paragraph{Reservoir unbiasedness for hypergraph triangle counting \citep{sigmod26-377}.}
Counting triangles in a hypergraph stream --- one-, two-, and three-hyperedge notions
alike --- is confounded by memory limits: the stream cannot be stored, so the count must
be estimated from a reservoir sample, and the estimator is only as good as the
reservoir's \emph{inclusion probabilities}. The paper's foundation is a reservoir lemma:
its partitioned sampler holds each hyperedge with equal probability $|G_s[i]|/m[i]$, so
each hyperedge seen so far is in the reservoir with probability $|G_s^{(t)}|/m^{(t)}$ at
\emph{every} time $t$ --- including after the replacement cascades that evictions force.
Unbiasedness is then immediate: each triangle's indicator, weighted by the inverse joint
inclusion probability of its hyperedges, has expectation equal to its presence, and
linearity sums to $\mathbb{E}[\hat c] = c$ (Theorems of the paper); direct variance
computations --- e.g.\ $\operatorname{Var}[\hat c_{\mathrm{otr}}] \le
(2c_{\mathrm{otr}}^2 - c_{\mathrm{otr}})\cdot
\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{|G_s|(|G_s|-1)(|G_s|-2)}$ --- complete the analysis. The lesson:
\emph{unbiasedness under sampling is a property of the sampler's inclusion
probabilities}, and maintaining them under memory pressure is itself a theorem.

\subsection{Further reading}

\begin{itemize}
\item Lehmann and Casella \citep{estimation-theory-bg1} --- the classical treatment of point
  estimation: unbiasedness, sufficiency, and the decision-theoretic view of MSE.
\item Tsybakov \citep{estimation-theory-bg2} --- minimax lower bounds (Le Cam, Fano); the
  source for the role our corpus papers fill with entropy and communication bounds.
\item Boucheron, Lugosi, and Massart \citep{estimation-theory-bg3} --- the non-asymptotic
  concentration machinery behind this chapter's rungs.
\item Mitzenmacher and Upfal \citep{estimation-theory-bg4} --- the randomized-algorithms view:
  sampling, Chernoff/Hoeffding, and the median trick, with CS applications.
\end{itemize}

\begin{recap}
\item \textbf{What the tool is.} Estimation theory certifies numbers a system did not exactly
  compute: an estimator $\hat\theta$ of a parameter $\theta$, its bias and variance
  ($\mathrm{MSE} = \text{bias}^2 + \mathrm{Var}$), and the $(E,\delta)$-accuracy contracts
  built from them by Chebyshev (samples $\propto 1/\delta$) or Hoeffding (samples $\propto
  \ln(\text{events}/\delta)$).
\item \textbf{Which rung when.} Inclusion probabilities known: Horvitz--Thompson weighting,
  unbiased by linearity. Heavy tails: truncate, pay the bias into the approximation factor.
  Only a variance bound: Chebyshev, boost by medians. Bounded range: Hoeffding, $t =
  r^2\ln(2M/\delta)/(2E^2)$ --- derive the sample count, never tune it. Nonlinear functional:
  estimate it directly per copy; unbiasedness does not survive plug-ins.
\item \textbf{Why unbiasedness first.} It makes errors add: independent unbiased pieces
  average to variance $\sigma^2/t$; one computation then feeds every concentration argument.
\item \textbf{What the corpus showed.} Noisy frequency moments (Section~\ref{sec:est-fp}):
  unbiased ordered-clique sampling, truncation, H\"older + Chebyshev + a reverse-Markov
  upper tail, DISJ near-optimality. Expandable sketches (Section~\ref{sec:est-sublime}):
  epoch-indexed variance recursion, Jensen-bounded encodings, an entropy lower bound.
  Biharmonic distance (Section~\ref{sec:est-bd}): Kirchhoff identities turn Laplacian
  entries into tree expectations; Hoeffding inversion outputs $\omega_t =
  d_v^2\Delta^2\log(2n/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2)$. Influence blocking
  (Section~\ref{sec:est-tcim}): unbiased coverage estimators plus Bernstein concentration
  setting the sample threshold and stopping rule; union bound $\Rightarrow$ $1-3\delta$.
\item \textbf{Advanced usage.} Anytime-valid betting confidence sequences make estimation a
  sequential stopping rule; reservoir inclusion-probability lemmas make unbiasedness a
  property of the sampler (Section~\ref{sec:est-advanced}). Deferred proofs live in the
  Appendix (Section~\ref{sec:est-appendix}).
\end{recap}

\section{Appendix}
\label{sec:est-appendix}

Proofs omitted from the main text are collected here.

\subsubsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:est-cr} (Cram\'er--Rao bound)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} For a regular family $p_\theta$ and any unbiased $\hat\theta$
with finite variance, $\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\hat\theta) \ge 1/I(\theta)$ with
$I(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_\theta[(\partial_\theta\log p_\theta(X))^2]$.\eop

\begin{proof}
Write $S(X) = \partial_\theta \log p_\theta(X)$ for the score. Regularity (support independent
of $\theta$; differentiation under the integral) gives $\mathbb{E}_\theta[S] = \partial_\theta
\int p_\theta = 0$, and, for unbiased $\hat\theta$,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(\hat\theta, S)
= \mathbb{E}_\theta[\hat\theta\,S]
= \int \hat\theta\,\partial_\theta p_\theta
= \partial_\theta \mathbb{E}_\theta[\hat\theta]
= \partial_\theta \theta = 1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz now gives
\[
1 \;=\; \operatorname{Cov}_\theta(\hat\theta,S)^2
\;\le\; \operatorname{Var}_\theta(\hat\theta)\cdot\operatorname{Var}_\theta(S)
\;=\; \operatorname{Var}_\theta(\hat\theta)\cdot I(\theta),
\]
since $\operatorname{Var}(S) = \mathbb{E}[S^2] = I(\theta)$. Divide by $I(\theta)$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:est-fp-trunc} (truncated clique estimator)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} With $I$ uniform on $[m]$, $X = m\,(d_I)^{p-1}$ --- a one-draw
form of the clique-mass estimator of Lemma~\ref{lem:est-fp-unbiased} --- and $Y = \min\{X,\,
m\,(d_I^\mu)^{p-1}\}$, we have $(1-2\Lambda_p)F_p(\mu) \le \mathbb{E}[Y] \le F_p(\mu)$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(Y) \le \mathbb{E}[Y^2] \le m^{1-1/p}F_p(\mu)^2$, where $d_i = |B_i^\sigma|$
and $d_i^\mu = |B_i^\mu|$.\eop

\begin{proof}
\emph{Upper expectation.} $Y \le m(d_I^\mu)^{p-1}$ with $I$ uniform, so $\mathbb{E}[Y] \le
\sum_i (d_i^\mu)^{p-1} = F_p(\mu)$, the last identity being the paper's Fact (ground-truth
cliques $V_1,\dots,V_{n_\mu}$ of sizes equal to the frequencies).

\emph{Lower expectation.} $\mathbb{E}[X-Y] = \frac1m\sum_i \max\bigl\{(d_i)^{p-1} -
(d_i^\mu)^{p-1},\,0\bigr\}$, and since $(d_i)^{p-1} \le |B_i^\sigma\cup B_i^\mu|^{p-1}$ while
$(d_i^\mu)^{p-1} \ge |B_i^\sigma\cap B_i^\mu|^{p-1}$, each summand is at most
$|B_i^\sigma\cup B_i^\mu|^{p-1} - |B_i^\sigma\cap B_i^\mu|^{p-1}$; summing gives
$\mathbb{E}[X-Y] \le \Lambda_p F_p(\mu)$, and the same display applied to
$|(d_i)^{p-1} - (d_i^\mu)^{p-1}|$ gives $\mathbb{E}[X] \ge (1-\Lambda_p)F_p(\mu)$. Hence
$\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E}[X] - \mathbb{E}[X-Y] \ge (1-2\Lambda_p)F_p(\mu)$.

\emph{Second moment.} $Y \le m\max_i (d_i^\mu)^{p-1} = m\max_k |V_k|^{p-1}$, so
$\mathbb{E}[Y^2] \le m\,F_p^{1-1/p}\cdot\mathbb{E}[Y] \le m\,F_p^{1-1/p}\cdot F_p$, using
$\max_k|V_k|^{p-1} \le F_p^{1-1/p}$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y] \le F_p$; and H\"older gives
$m = \sum_k |V_k| \le n_\mu^{1-1/p}F_p^{1/p}$ with $n_\mu \le m$, so $\mathbb{E}[Y^2] \le
m^{1-1/p}F_p(\mu)^2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(Y) \le \mathbb{E}[Y^2]$. (This already
suffices for Theorem~\ref{thm:est-fp-upper}: Chebyshev on $t$ copies gives failure
$m^{1-1/p}/(t\varepsilon^2) \le 10^{-5}$ at $t = \Theta(\varepsilon^{-2}m^{1-1/p})$.)
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:est-bd-fasttree} (tree-sample size)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} With $\omega_t = d_v^2\Delta^2\log(2n/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2)$,
the averaged tree estimators satisfy $|\tilde x_a(u) - x_a(u)| \le \varepsilon$ and
$|\tilde x_b(u) - x_b(u)| \le \varepsilon$ for every node $u$ simultaneously with probability
$1-\delta$.\eop

\begin{proof}
The per-tree contributions are bounded: $|\tilde x_b^i(u)| \le 2\Delta$ (paths from $s$ and
from $t$) and $|\tilde x_a^i(u)| \le d_v\Delta$ (range bounds stated with the estimators,
Corollary~6.2 of the paper; at most $d_v$ neighbor paths feed $\mathbf{x}_a$), and they are
i.i.d.\ with means $x_a(u)$, $x_b(u)$. Additive Hoeffding on $\omega_t$ i.i.d.\ copies with
range widths $r_a = 2d_v\Delta$ and $r_b = 4\Delta$ gives
\[
\Pr\bigl[|\tilde x_a(u) - x_a(u)| \ge \varepsilon\bigr] \;\le\;
2\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{2\omega_t\varepsilon^2}{4d_v^2\Delta^2}\Bigr) \;\le\; \frac{\delta}{2n},
\]
since $\omega_t = d_v^2\Delta^2\log(2n/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2)$ makes the exponent exactly
$\log(2n/\delta)$; similarly $\Pr[|\tilde x_b(u) - x_b(u)| \ge \varepsilon] \le
2\exp(-2\omega_t\varepsilon^2/(16\Delta^2)) \le \delta/(2n)$, absorbing the constant into the
logarithm. A union bound over the $n$ nodes and the two coordinate families keeps every
deviation below $\varepsilon$ with probability $1-\delta$. The running time is $\omega_t$ times
one tree's cost: Wilson's algorithm runs in time proportional to the hitting times from the
roots $\{s,t\}\cup N(v)$ to $v$.
\end{proof}
