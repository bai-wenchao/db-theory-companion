\chapter{Differential Privacy: Composition and its Calculus}
\setcounter{section}{-1}   % sections start at 6.0 (see main.tex comment)
\label{ch:dp}

\begin{objectives}
\item Recognize when a system's privacy proof is a \emph{ledger}: several releases, each with
  its own budget, that must add up.
\item Prove the ladder sequential $\to$ parallel $\to$ post-processing $\to$ group privacy,
  and match each rung to the data-access structure.
\item Calibrate every release: compute the sensitivity, split the budget, then pick
  Laplace or exponential-mechanism noise.
\item Dissect a paper's end-to-end proof: which rung carries each stage, and where budgets
  add rather than maximize.
\end{objectives}

A data system rarely releases one number. A private analytics engine answers many
queries; an explanation tool selects candidates, then scores them, then publishes
histograms; a join algorithm's \emph{access pattern} is itself a leak. Differential
privacy makes each single release safe, but the contract the auditor signs is
end-to-end: everything the system ever emits must jointly satisfy one
indistinguishability guarantee. The \emph{composition calculus} is the small algebra
that converts per-release budgets into that single contract: budgets of releases on
the same data add (sequential composition); releases on disjoint slices cost only the
largest budget (parallel composition); anything computed \emph{after} a private
release is free (post-processing immunity); and a guarantee against one-record changes
degrades linearly when the adversary's inputs differ by a whole group (group privacy).
Around this core sits the calibration rule that decides what a single release costs
in the first place: sensitivity $+$ calibrated noise, the Laplace and exponential
mechanisms.

In the SIGMOD'26 corpus the tool is close to a monoculture: among the 26 papers tagged
\texttt{privacy-dp}, 18 assemble their end-to-end privacy guarantee by composing
mechanisms (69\%), and the pairing \texttt{privacy-guarantee} $\times$
\texttt{dp-composition} is the tool's strongest, with 18 papers. This chapter builds
the ladder and walks four full exemplars through it;
Section~\ref{sec:dp-advanced} sketches two harder uses (oblivious joins, private
shortest paths) and points to the literature.

\section{The tool: DP composition}
\label{sec:dp-tool}

The problem class is uniform: a mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ must publish several interacting
statistics of a dataset $D$ --- selections, counts, noisy parameters, even the sequence of
memory cells it touches --- and certify one contract over the \emph{joint} output. The
contract is indistinguishability of neighboring inputs:

\begin{definition}[Differential privacy]\label{def:dp-dp}
A randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differential
privacy if for all \emph{neighboring} inputs $D \sim D'$ and all measurable sets $S$ of
outputs,
\[
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\,\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] \;+\; \delta .
\]
With $\delta = 0$ the guarantee is \emph{pure} $\varepsilon$-DP
\citep{dp-composition-bg1,dp-composition-bg3}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remarkbox}[Notation]
Neighboring means ``one unit apart'' in whichever granularity the contract names: one
tuple added or removed (the relational default; the \emph{unbounded} setting, versus
bounded when a size cap is public), one edge (edge-DP), or one node with all its incident
edges (node-DP) --- Section~\ref{sec:dp-337} lives entirely in the gap between the last
two. Typical calibrations keep $\varepsilon$ a constant and $\delta \le 1/N$ for $N$ data
subjects. $\operatorname{Lap}(b)$ is the Laplace distribution of scale $b$, and
$\operatorname{Lap}$ noise is added independently per coordinate.
\end{remarkbox}

What a single release costs is governed by how much one unit of input change can move it:

\begin{definition}[Global sensitivity]\label{def:dp-sens}
For a query $Q$ on datasets, the \emph{global sensitivity} is
\[
GS_Q \;=\; \max_{D \sim D'} \;\bigl|Q(D) - Q(D')\bigr|,
\]
the maximum over neighboring pairs, in the norm of the output space.
\end{definition}

Counting queries have $GS_Q = 1$; a sum over values in $[0,U]$ has $GS_Q = U$; a
histogram has sensitivity $1$ in $\ell_1$. Two mechanisms convert sensitivities into
releases:

\begin{theorem}[Laplace mechanism]\label{thm:dp-laplace}
Let $Q$ have global sensitivity $GS_Q$. Then
$\mathcal{M}(D) = Q(D) + \operatorname{Lap}(GS_Q/\varepsilon)$ (i.i.d.\ noise per
coordinate) satisfies $\varepsilon$-DP. \eop
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For any output $y$, the ratio of densities at $y$ under the two neighbors is at most
$\exp(|Q(D)-Q(D')|/b) = e^{GS_Q/b} = e^{\varepsilon}$ with $b = GS_Q/\varepsilon$;
integrating over $y \in S$ preserves the bound (full proof in
Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}).
\end{proof}

For selecting a discrete object (an attribute, a plan, a candidate set), noise per
coordinate is replaced by a biased lottery:

\begin{theorem}[Exponential mechanism]\label{thm:dp-em}
Given candidates $\mathcal{R}$ and a quality function $q(D,r)$ of sensitivity
$\Delta_q$, the mechanism selecting $r \in \mathcal{R}$ with probability proportional to
$\exp(\varepsilon \cdot q(D,r) / (2\Delta_q))$ satisfies $\varepsilon$-DP. Moreover the
selected $r_E$ is nearly optimal:
\[
\Pr\bigl[\,q(D,r_E) \;\le\; \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} q(D,r) - 2\Delta_q
\bigl(\ln|\mathcal{R}| + t\bigr)\bigr] \;\le\; e^{-t}. \eopm
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each candidate's selection-probability ratio between neighbors is at most
$e^{\varepsilon}$ because $q$ moves by at most $\Delta_q$ and the exponent normalizes
by $2\Delta_q$; the utility clause integrates the tail of the same lottery. (Statement
as restated in \citep{sigmod26-298}, which cites the original.)
\end{proof}

These are the coins; the calculus is how coins combine. The workhorse is sequential
composition --- note that it covers \emph{adaptive} pipelines, where later mechanisms see
earlier outputs:

\begin{theorem}[Sequential composition]\label{thm:dp-seq}
Let $\mathcal{M}_1\colon \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{R}_1$ satisfy
$(\varepsilon_1,\delta_1)$-DP and let $\mathcal{M}_2\colon \mathcal{D} \times
\mathcal{R}_1 \to \mathcal{R}_2$ satisfy $(\varepsilon_2,\delta_2)$-DP in its first
input for \emph{every} fixed value of the second. Then
$\mathcal{M}_3(D) = \mathcal{M}_2(D, \mathcal{M}_1(D))$ satisfies
$(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2,\ \delta_1+\delta_2)$-DP; iterating, $k$ adaptive releases
satisfy $(\sum_i \varepsilon_i,\ \sum_i \delta_i)$-DP. \eop
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Condition on $\mathcal{M}_1$'s output $y$: each conditional law obeys its own bound, and
integrating over $y$ multiplies the $e^{\varepsilon_i}$ factors and adds the
$\delta_i$'s (full proof in Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}).
\end{proof}

When releases touch \emph{disjoint} parts of the data, the arithmetic improves from sums
to maxima:

\begin{theorem}[Parallel composition]\label{thm:dp-parallel}
Let $\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2$ satisfy $(\varepsilon_1,\delta_1)$- and
$(\varepsilon_2,\delta_2)$-DP, and let $D_1, D_2$ be disjoint subsets of the input
domain. Then $\mathcal{M}'(D) = (\mathcal{M}_1(D_{D_1}), \mathcal{M}_2(D_{D_2}))$ ---
each mechanism seeing only the records of its slice --- satisfies
$(\max\{\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\},\ \max\{\delta_1,\delta_2\})$-DP. \eop
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A unit input change lands in exactly one slice, say $D_1$; the other mechanism's output
distribution is then identical under both neighbors, so only the $\varepsilon_1$ bound
survives the product-measure factorization.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-seq} charges for every release; the next rule charges for none:

\begin{theorem}[Post-processing immunity]\label{thm:dp-post}
If $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP and $g$ is any (deterministic or
randomized) function that does not touch the raw data, then $g(\mathcal{M}(D))$
satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP. \eop
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Disintegrate $g$ against the output law: $\Pr[g(\mathcal{M}(D)) \in T] =
\int \Pr[g(y) \in T]\, d\mathcal{M}(D)(y) \le e^{\varepsilon}
\int \Pr[g(y) \in T]\, d\mathcal{M}(D')(y) + \delta$.
\end{proof}

\sidenote{Post-processing immunity is what licenses \emph{system artifacts} as private
objects: clipped graphs (Section~\ref{sec:dp-337}), pruned indices, and even access
patterns of algorithms whose memory layout is a function of a private release
(Section~\ref{sec:dp-advanced}). No cleverness \emph{after} the release can repair a
broken release, either.}

When the two inputs differ by a whole group of $\lambda$ units, the guarantee degrades
linearly:

\begin{theorem}[Group privacy]\label{thm:dp-group}
If $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP, then for any two inputs
$G, G'$ at neighborhood distance $\lambda$ and any output set $S$,
\[
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G) \in S] \;\le\; e^{\lambda\varepsilon}\,
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G') \in S] \;+\; \lambda\delta. \eopm
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Insert $\lambda$ intermediates $G = G_0 \sim G_1 \sim \dots \sim G_\lambda = G'$ and
telescope the DP bound along the chain; the $\delta$ terms accumulate geometrically and
are absorbed by $\lambda e^{(\lambda-1)\varepsilon}\delta \ge \lambda\delta$
(Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}).
\end{proof}

Plain summation is not the end of the story: $k$ adaptive releases can be accounted
far better than $k\varepsilon$, because the privacy-loss random variables concentrate
(Chapter~\ref{ch:conc}):

\begin{theorem}[Advanced composition]\label{thm:dp-advanced}
For $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0,1)$ and $\delta' \in (0,1)$, the $k$-fold adaptive
composition of $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP mechanisms satisfies
$(\varepsilon',\ k\delta + \delta')$-DP for
\[
\varepsilon' \;=\; \sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}\cdot\bigl(e^{\varepsilon}-1\bigr)
\;\le\; 2\varepsilon\sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}. \eopm
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Write the log-likelihood ratio of outputs along the adaptive chain; each increment is
bounded and (near-)centered, so Azuma--Hoeffding concentrates the total loss at
$O(\varepsilon\sqrt{k})$ with failure $\delta'$ \citep{dp-composition-bg2}. R\'enyi DP
composes the same loss in moments and converts back
\citep{dp-composition-bg4,dp-composition-bg5}; subsampling buys a further discount
\citep{dp-composition-bg6}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{How to compose a privacy proof}
\label{sec:dp-howto}

Composing an end-to-end proof is mechanical once the system is drawn as the DAG of
Figure~\ref{fig:dp-ledger}. \emph{First}, enumerate every channel that touches the raw
data: released statistics, private hyperparameters that steer later computation, and
--- in oblivious settings --- access patterns. \emph{Second}, fix the neighborhood each
channel is analyzed under and compute its query's sensitivity; a wrong neighborhood
(edge when the contract says node) silently breaks the ledger. \emph{Third},
instantiate each channel with the mechanism matching its output type: Laplace for
numeric aggregates, the exponential mechanism for selections, the sparse vector
technique for threshold searches (Section~\ref{sec:dp-337}). \emph{Fourth}, read off
the total: sums along adaptive chains, maxima across disjoint slices, linear inflation
across groups; post-processing edges carry zero. Budgets are \emph{split}, not
discovered: fix the total $\varepsilon$ first and allocate shares so no single stage
dominates the error.

\begin{reachbox}{When to Reach for DP Composition}
Reach whenever a system releases \emph{several} interacting statistics --- candidate
selections, noisy summaries, clipped releases, adaptive searches --- and the
deliverable is one end-to-end privacy contract. Then run the recipe:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the releases: every mechanism that reads raw data, including private
  parameters that steer later stages and observable access patterns.
\item Split the budget: fix the target $(\varepsilon,\delta)$, allocate
  $(\varepsilon_i,\delta_i)$ per release; spend less on stages whose error matters
  less.
\item Compute each query's sensitivity under the neighborhood the contract names
  (tuple/edge/node; add/remove); calibrate Laplace or exponential-mechanism noise to
  it.
\item Compose: add along adaptive chains, take the maximum over disjoint data slices,
  multiply by group size when converting between neighborhoods; union-bound the
  failure probabilities over stages.
\item Post-process freely: clipping, rounding, index building, and all downstream
  analytics ride on the released values at zero cost --- but never re-touch the raw
  data.
\end{enumerate}
Skip the machinery when a single calibrated release suffices: composing there only
overcounts. With many ($k \gtrsim 50$) releases, plain summation goes vacuous ---
switch the ledger to advanced or moment accounting (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-advanced}).
\end{reachbox}

\section{Private explanations for clusters}
\label{sec:dp-298}

The exemplar is a SIGMOD'26 paper on explainable clustering under privacy
\citep{sigmod26-298}. Clustering is a workhorse of exploratory data analysis, but a bare
cluster label tells a stakeholder nothing: analysts want an \emph{explanation} --- which
attribute combination accounts for the partition, e.g.\ that customers grouped together
because of age bracket and spending band. Computing such explanations requires scanning
the tuples inside each cluster, and the explanation itself (``cluster~3 is explained by
salary'') leaks information about those individuals, so it must be privatized like any
other release. Prior private explanation tools could explain one cluster at a time but
not certify a \emph{global} assignment --- one attribute per cluster, jointly chosen
--- under a single privacy budget. The paper builds the first mechanism for such global
histogram-based explanations (HBE), combining top-$k$ candidate selection, an
exponential mechanism over combinations, and noisy per-cluster histograms, and proves an
end-to-end budget that is exactly the sum of three named dials. By direct sensitivity
analysis it also shows that the standard divergence scores used in explanation systems
are \emph{high-sensitivity}, and replaces them with low-sensitivity surrogates so each
dial can be calibrated tightly.

\paragraph{Problem.}
A dataset $D$ and a clustering function $f$ partitioning the tuples into clusters
$D_c$, $c \in C$, are given; $f$ is already public. A \emph{single-cluster HBE
candidate} is a tuple $(c, A, h_A(D \setminus D_c), h_A(D_c))$ pairing cluster $c$ with
an explanatory attribute $A$ and two histograms; a \emph{global HBE candidate} assigns
one such candidate per cluster. Quality is scored by an \emph{interestingness} (how
skewed $A$'s distribution is inside $c$ relative to the rest of $D$), a
\emph{sufficiency}, and a \emph{diversity} score (how distinct the chosen attributes
are across clusters), combined by convex weights $\lambda$ (summing to one) into a
global score $\psi_\lambda(D, f, \mathbf{e})$. The mechanism must choose a global
candidate and publish supporting histograms under pure DP, neighbors $D \sim D'$
differing by one tuple.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[Privacy of the global explanation, Theorem of the paper]\label{thm:dp-298-main}
Given a clustering function $f$, a number of candidates $k$, and privacy parameters
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CandSet}}, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TopComb}},
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Hist}}$, the algorithm \textsc{gen\_global\_explanation} is
$(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CandSet}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TopComb}} +
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Hist}})$-DP. \eop
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How DP composition is applied.}
The proof is the ledger of Figure~\ref{fig:dp-ledger} instantiated stage by stage. The
top-$k$ candidate selection is realized as $|C| \cdot k$ iterated exponential
mechanisms, each at budget $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CandSet}}/(|C| k)$; sequential
composition (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-seq}) totals $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CandSet}}$. The
combination selection is one exponential mechanism (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-em}) at unit
sensitivity, costing $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TopComb}}$. The histogram stage splits
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Hist}}$ in half: per-attribute histograms all read the full
dataset, so their budgets add sequentially to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Hist}}/2$;
per-cluster histograms read \emph{disjoint} clusters, so parallel composition
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-parallel}) caps their total at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Hist}}/2$ ---
maxima, not sums. The complementary histograms $\tilde h^{-c}$ are computed from
already-released values, so post-processing immunity (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-post}) makes
them free; summing the three stages gives the theorem. The unit-sensitivity input to
the exponential mechanism is itself the paper's workhorse lemma:

\begin{lemma}[Convex combinations of scores, Lemma of the paper]\label{lem:dp-298-convex}
For $i = 1,\dots,m$ let $\alpha_i \ge 0$ be scalars and $f_i$ functions of sensitivity
$\Delta_i$. Then $f = \sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_i f_i$ has sensitivity at most
$\sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_i \Delta_i$. \eop
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For neighboring $D \sim D'$, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
\bigl|f(D) - f(D')\bigr|
\;&=\;\Bigl|\textstyle\sum_i \alpha_i f_i(D) - \sum_i \alpha_i f_i(D')\Bigr|\\
\;&\le\; \sum_i \alpha_i \bigl|f_i(D) - f_i(D')\bigr|
\;\le\; \sum_i \alpha_i \Delta_i. \eopm
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

Since $\psi_\lambda$ is a convex combination (weights summing to one) of three
sensitivity-$\le 1$ scores, Lemma~\ref{lem:dp-298-convex} bounds its sensitivity by $1$,
and the exponential mechanism runs at exactly one unit. The sensitivity-$1$ bounds are
direct $\ell_1$ histogram algebra: for the interestingness score
$\iota(D,f,c,A) = \tfrac12 \bigl\| h_A(D_c) - \tfrac{|D_c|}{|D|} h_A(D) \bigr\|_1$,
adding one tuple adds a unit basis vector $h_a$ ($\|h_a\|_1 = 1$) to one histogram and
rescales the other by $\|h_A(D)\|_1 = |D|$; a two-case analysis (tuple inside vs.\
outside the cluster) totals at most $1$ in both cases. The replacement is justified by
proving the \emph{standard} scores cannot do better:

\begin{proposition}[Jensen--Shannon sensitivity, Proposition of the paper, notation
cleaned]\label{prop:dp-298-jsd}
The Jensen--Shannon distance $d_{JS}$, used as a diversity score on neighboring
datasets, has sensitivity at least $\tfrac12$: there are neighbors $D \sim D'$ with
$|d_{JS}(D) - d_{JS}(D')| > \tfrac12$. \eop
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Take $D$ where every tuple carries value $a$ on attribute $A$ and a singleton cluster
$D_c = \{t\}$; both distributions are point masses, so the score is $0$. Add one tuple
$t'$ with a fresh value $a'$: the dataset distribution becomes
$(\tfrac{n}{n+1}, \tfrac{1}{n+1})$ and the cluster's becomes
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. The Jensen--Shannon divergence works out to
$H_b\bigl(\tfrac{n}{2(n+1)} + \tfrac14\bigr) - \tfrac12 H_b\bigl(\tfrac{1}{n+1}\bigr)
- \tfrac12 H_b\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr) \to H_b(\tfrac14) - \tfrac12 \approx 0.311$ bits,
so the distance exceeds $\sqrt{0.311} > \tfrac12$ for large $n$ (full computation in
Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}).
\end{proof}

The same binary-entropy limit holds for the total-variation interestingness of prior
work, so no divergence-based score can shrink the noise --- the sensitivity-$1$
surrogates are within a factor of two of optimal \emph{and} admit the clean algebra
above.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
The deployed system gets a private explanation engine whose contract is three readable
dials --- candidates, combination, histograms --- each independently tunable;
parallel composition across disjoint clusters means adding clusters costs no histogram
budget; and the exponential mechanism's utility theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-em})
converts the unit sensitivity directly into near-optimal explanation quality.

\section{Optimal pure-DP sparse histograms in deterministic linear time}
\label{sec:dp-178}

The exemplar is a PODS'26 paper on private histogram release \citep{sigmod26-178}\pods.
Releasing a histogram --- how many users hold each value --- is the most basic private
analytics primitive, and it is delicate when the domain $[d]$ is enormous but any real
dataset touches few values: the mechanism must publish a \emph{sparse} histogram whose
support tracks the data, not the domain. Two older constraints sat uneasily together:
the samplers that produce pure $\varepsilon$-DP noise (no $\delta$ slack) have
data-dependent running times, and the known linear-time mechanisms paid with
randomized- or approximate-DP only --- so timing side channels or approximate
guarantees leaked in through the implementation. The paper closes both gaps at once: a
pure-DP mechanism that releases a histogram over $n$ users and domain $[d]$ in
\emph{deterministic} $O(n)$ time in the word-RAM model, with per-coordinate error
asymptotically matching the known lower bound of Balcer and Vadhan. The privacy proof
is a case-by-case dominance analysis over the supports of neighboring histograms,
composed with a private upper-bound search.

\paragraph{Problem.}
The input is a dataset of $n$ user-contributed elements from a domain $[d]$, viewed as
its histogram $h \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^d$; neighbors differ by one added or removed
element (the unbounded setting, so the dataset size itself is sensitive). The
mechanism must output a \emph{sparse} $\hat h$ ($\|\hat h\|_0 \le O(n)$) approximating
$h$ in every coordinate simultaneously under pure DP, in deterministic linear time ---
the running time may depend only on $n$, never on which values the users hold.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[Private sparse histogram, informal version of Theorem 6.1 of the
paper]\label{thm:dp-178-main}
Let $n, d \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, with representations that fit in a
constant number of machine words. Given a dataset of $n$ elements from $[d]$, there is
an $\varepsilon$-differentially private algorithm that runs deterministically in $O(n)$
time and outputs a histogram $\hat h$ with $\|\hat h\|_0 \le O(n)$ and
$\ell_\infty$ error asymptotically optimal --- matching the lower bound
$\Omega\bigl(\min\{\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln d,\ n\}\bigr)$ of Balcer and Vadhan quoted
in the paper:
\[
\|\hat h - h\|_\infty \;=\; O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln d\Bigr). \eopm
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How DP composition is applied.}
The noise source is the discrete cousin of the Laplace mechanism
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-laplace}): a \emph{geometric} (discrete Laplace) variable with
per-coordinate tail
\[
\Pr[X \ge t] \;\le\; \frac{2\,e^{-\varepsilon t}}{1 + e^{-\varepsilon}}
\qquad (t \ge 1)
\]
(Fact of the paper, notation cleaned). A union bound over the $d$ coordinates turns the
per-coordinate tail into the simultaneous accuracy
$\alpha = (c_\alpha/\varepsilon)\ln(d/\beta)$ at confidence $1-\beta$ --- the
$(\alpha,\beta)$-simultaneous estimator of the formal theorem. The sampler itself is
engineered to be \emph{timing-oblivious}, with an indistinguishability contract of its
own (the ``purified'' relaxed discrete Laplace sampler, informal Theorem of the paper):
for each $t \in [n]$, $\Gamma(t-1)$ and $\Gamma(t)$ are
$(\varepsilon,0)$-indistinguishable, and for $\beta > 2\gamma$, $\Gamma(t)$ estimates
$t$ to within $\alpha = O\bigl(\tfrac1\varepsilon \ln\tfrac1\beta\bigr)$ with
probability $1-\beta$. Privacy of the histogram is \emph{not} a one-line sensitivity
argument, because neighboring datasets have different supports: writing $i^*$ and $j^*$
for the heaviest coordinates of $h$ and $h'$ and $I_1, I_1'$ for the two sampled
supports, the output distributions $\mu, \mu'$ are compared in \emph{four cases}
according to which of $i^*, j^*$ lie in $I_1, I_1'$; in each case the mixture
decomposes over the shared support and the per-coordinate dominance ratio
$r_J \in (e^{-\varepsilon/2}, e^{\varepsilon/2})$ yields
$\mu'(S) \le e^{\varepsilon}\mu(S)$. Finally, because the setting is unbounded, the
algorithm first runs a private \emph{upper-bound search} on the dataset size (an
$\varepsilon_1$-DP time-oblivious search quoting prior work, restated in the paper),
truncates the input, and pads with the $\varepsilon_2$-parameterized geometric
mechanism; the paper quotes basic sequential composition
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-seq}) to combine the two stages into the stated pure budget.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
Analytics stacks needing per-value counts --- contingency tables, heavy-hitter
dashboards, training features --- get a black-box release whose running time is
worst-case, so the implementation leaks nothing through timing; the optimal
$\ell_\infty$ certificate means the noise is provably not improvable, and the sparse
support keeps storage at $O(n)$ regardless of the domain.

\section{Reducing node-DP to edge-DP}
\label{sec:dp-337}

The exemplar is a SIGMOD'26 paper on private graph analytics \citep{sigmod26-337}.
Publishing graph statistics --- triangle counts, degree moments, subgraph counts ---
under differential privacy requires choosing the unit of protection: \emph{edge-DP}
hides one edge, while \emph{node-DP} hides one vertex together with all its incident
edges --- the right contract when vertices are people. Node-DP is strictly harder: a
single high-degree vertex can shift many statistics, so sensitivities explode with the
maximum degree, and existing node-DP techniques mostly assume a predefined degree
bound. Setting that bound conservatively destroys utility; the paper quotes the naive
escape --- dividing the budget by the bound via group privacy --- as exactly this
failure mode. The N2E framework instead \emph{privately learns} a degree bound from the
graph itself, clips the graph to it, and runs any off-the-shelf edge-DP mechanism on the
clipped graph, converting the whole edge toolbox to node-DP with error governed by the
data's actual degree structure. The end-to-end proof is sequential composition over
three steps, with group privacy and post-processing doing the conversion.

\paragraph{Problem.}
Work on graphs with two neighborhood relations: $G \sim_{\mathrm{edge}} G'$ differ by
one edge, $G \sim_{\mathrm{node}} G'$ by one node and its incident edges;
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP is Definition~\ref{def:dp-dp} under either. A
\emph{clipping} $\mathrm{Clip}(G,\Delta)$ keeps, for each node, its $\Delta$
smallest-order edges. Given any graph task $Q$ and any edge-DP mechanism
$\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{edge}}_Q$ for it, N2E must output a node-DP estimate of $Q(G)$
whose error degrades with the true maximum degree $\mathit{deg}(G)$, not a worst-case
bound $N$.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[N2E accuracy and privacy, Theorems of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{thm:dp-337-main}
Given any $\varepsilon, \beta, \delta$, for any graph $G$, task $Q$, and edge-DP
mechanism $\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{edge}}_Q$: with probability at least $1-\beta$, N2E
outputs a node-DP result with error
\[
\bigl|Q(G) - Q(\tilde G)\bigr| \;+\;
\operatorname{Err}^{\mathrm{edge}}_Q\bigl(\tilde G,\;
\varepsilon/(6\Delta^*),\; \delta/(4\Delta^*),\; \beta/3\bigr),
\]
where $\tilde G$ is obtained by clipping the edges of
$O\bigl(\log(\log(\mathit{deg}(G))/\beta)/\varepsilon\bigr)$ nodes of $G$, and the
framework as a whole satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-node-DP. \eop
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How DP composition is applied.}
The framework is a three-step ledger. \emph{Step 1} privately approximates the maximum
degree by a search over decreasing thresholds $\Delta$, at budget
$(2\varepsilon/3, \delta/2)$; \emph{step 2}, the clipping, consumes no budget --- it is
post-processing (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-post}) of $\Delta^*$; \emph{step 3} runs the edge
mechanism at $\varepsilon/(6\Delta^*)$ and $\delta/(4\Delta^*)$. Group privacy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-group}) is the rung doing the reduction: a node change becomes at
most $\lambda \le 2\Delta^*$ edge changes after clipping (with probability
$1-\delta/2$, by a clipping-distance lemma below), so an edge-DP mechanism at budget
$\varepsilon/\lambda$ is node-DP at $\varepsilon/3$. Sequential composition
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-seq}) totals the steps to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$. The degree
search is a sparse-vector-technique (SVT) instance whose queries are chosen to have
unit sensitivity:

\begin{lemma}[SVT over sensitivity-monotonic queries, Lemma of the paper, restating
prior work]\label{lem:dp-337-svt}
If the query sequence used in SVT is sensitivity-monotonic, then with threshold
calibration $c=1$ SVT preserves $\varepsilon$-DP. Moreover, given failure probability
$\beta$, if some query satisfies $Q_k(G) \ge T + 4\ln(2k/\beta)/\varepsilon$, then with
probability at least $1-\beta$ SVT returns an index $\hat\imath \le k$ with
$Q_{\hat\imath}(G) \ge T - 4\ln(2k/\beta)/\varepsilon$. \eop
\end{lemma}

The query family is $Q_{\mathrm{Del\text{-}N}}(G,\Delta)$ --- minus the number of
nodes that must be deleted to cap the degree at $\Delta$ --- which the paper shows is
monotone in $\Delta$, has global sensitivity $1$ under node-DP, and is
\emph{sensitivity-monotonic} (neighboring graphs order its values consistently): the
three properties SVT needs. Because the search evaluates $\Delta$ on a
\emph{logarithmic} scale, only $\ln\log(4\,\mathit{deg}(G))$ iterations are ever
consumed. For tasks where node deletion is combinatorial, the paper relaxes it to an
LP over variables $(x_v, y_e)$ --- $x_v$ = delete node $v$, $y_e$ = drop edge $e$ ---
whose optimum $Q_{\mathrm{LP\text{-}Del\text{-}N}}(G,\Delta)$ keeps the same three
properties, and rounds:

\begin{lemma}[LP rounding, Lemma of the paper]\label{lem:dp-337-lp}
For any graph $G$ and any $\Delta$, the LP optimum upper-bounds the deletion query in
the sense that
$|Q_{\mathrm{Del\text{-}N}}(G, 3\Delta)| \;\le\; 3\,|Q_{\mathrm{LP\text{-}Del\text{-}N}}(G,\Delta)|$. \eop
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From the optimal LP solution, keep every edge with $x_v, x_{v'} \le \tfrac13$ (so
$y_e \ge 1 - x_v - x_{v'} \ge \tfrac13$) and delete every node with $x_v > \tfrac13$
(paying $n_v \le 3x_v$ per node). Each retained node's degree is at most
$3\sum_{e \ni v} y_e \le 3\Delta$, and the number of deletions is at most
$3\sum_v x_v = 3|Q_{\mathrm{LP\text{-}Del\text{-}N}}(G,\Delta)|$ --- a feasible
deletion scheme at threshold $3\Delta$.
\end{proof}

The remaining ingredient --- that clipping does not explode the edge distance --- is the
paper's clipping lemma; since a node change can saturate many nodes, the proof bounds
the two directions of edge difference separately (deferred to
Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}):

\begin{lemma}[Clipping distance, Lemma of the paper, notation cleaned]
\label{lem:dp-337-clip}
Let $G \subseteq G'$ be neighboring under node addition, and let $k$ be the number of
nodes of $G$ with degree at least $\Delta$. Then the edge distance between
$\mathrm{Clip}(G,\Delta)$ and $\mathrm{Clip}(G',\Delta)$ is at most $\Delta + k$. \eop
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{What it buys.}
Every edge-DP graph algorithm becomes node-DP --- protecting individuals rather than
single relationships --- by plugging it into N2E, with error tracking the actual
degree profile through the $O(\log(\log(\mathit{deg}(G))/\beta)/\varepsilon)$-node
clipping overhead instead of a worst-case bound; the LP-rounded variant keeps the
framework polynomial-time even when the exact deletion query is NP-hard.

\section{Per-record differential privacy}
\label{sec:dp-263}

The exemplar is a SIGMOD'26 paper on heterogeneous privacy budgets \citep{sigmod26-263}.
Classical DP charges every record the same $\varepsilon$, but real deployments often
want per-record budgets: a bank may owe wealthier clients stronger protection, and
regulations may grade protection by record type. This \emph{per-record DP} (PrDP)
replaces the scalar $\varepsilon$ with a budget function
$\varepsilon_\mathcal{E}\colon [U]^d \to [\varepsilon_{\min}, \varepsilon_{\max}]$ ---
now the privacy contract itself is a \emph{function}, and so is the composition
arithmetic. The paper gives a general framework: privately discard the records whose
budgets are too small to be worth noise, then run any existing DP mechanism on the
survivors at the minimum surviving budget. For sums and counts it builds direct
mechanisms that partition records into geometrically doubling budget slices, one
Laplace mechanism per slice, and proves them near-optimal against down-neighborhood
lower bounds --- the same yardstick style as Section~\ref{sec:dp-337}.

\paragraph{Problem.}
Records come from a universe $[U]^d$; a budget function $\varepsilon_\mathcal{E}$
assigns each record $r$ a budget in $[\varepsilon_{\min}, \varepsilon_{\max}]$ (for
the bank: a constant factor times wealth, capped). The \emph{privacy-specified domain}
$X_{[\varepsilon_a, \varepsilon_b]} = \{r : \varepsilon_\mathcal{E}(r) \in
[\varepsilon_a, \varepsilon_b]\}$ collects records whose budgets fall in a band. The
contract: $\mathcal{M}$ is $\varepsilon_\mathcal{E}$-PrDP if for all neighbors
$D \sim_r D'$ (differing in record $r$) and all outputs $y$,
$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) = y] \le e^{\varepsilon_\mathcal{E}(r)}\,
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') = y]$. Queries of interest are sums and counts.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}

\begin{theorem}[General framework, Theorem of the paper]\label{thm:dp-263-main}
For any budget function $\varepsilon_\mathcal{E}(\cdot)$, $\beta \in (0,1)$, and any DP
mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ for query $Q$, the framework returns $\hat Q(D)$ such that, with
probability at least $1-\beta$,
\[
\bigl|\hat Q(D) - Q(D)\bigr| \;\le\;
\underbrace{\bigl|Q(D) - Q(D')\bigr|}_{\text{(I)}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\operatorname{Err}_{\mathcal{M}}\bigl(D',\;
\varepsilon_{\min}(D)/4,\; \beta/2\bigr)}_{\text{(II)}},
\]
where $D'$ is obtained by removing
$O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{\min}(D)}\log\log(\cdot/\beta)\bigr)$ records from $D$
(hiding the argument of the iterated logarithm), and $\varepsilon_{\min}(D)$ is the
smallest budget among records of $D$. \eop
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How DP composition is applied.}
Term~(I) is a \emph{bias} term --- the query change caused by discarding the budget-poor
records --- and term~(II) is the noise of the black-box mechanism run on the survivors:
a sensitivity-vs-truncation trade, as in Section~\ref{sec:dp-337} but with budgets, not
degrees, deciding the cut. To locate the cut, the count estimation searches over the
doubling partition $X_i = X_{[2^{i-1}\varepsilon_a,\ 2^i\varepsilon_a)}$: it releases
per-slice counts with slice-specific Laplace noise, thresholds them (Laplace tail
bounds plus a union bound over the $\log(\varepsilon_{\max}/\varepsilon_{\min})$
slices), and certifies that the first surviving slice has budget
$\hat\varepsilon \ge \tfrac12\varepsilon_{\min}(D)$ while the discarded slices together
hold only $O(\tfrac1{\varepsilon_{\min}(D)} \log\log(\cdot/\beta))$ records --- the
failure budget $\beta$ split per slice and union-bounded, exactly the recipe of
Section~\ref{sec:dp-howto}. The sum estimation composes per-slice mechanisms, with the
composition arithmetic lifted from scalars to functions:

\begin{lemma}[PrDP parallel composition, Lemma of the paper]\label{lem:dp-263-parallel}
Let $R_1,\dots,R_k$ be disjoint subdomains and $\mathcal{M}_1,\dots,\mathcal{M}_k$
$\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}_i}$-PrDP mechanisms. Their parallel composition
$\mathcal{M}(D) := (\mathcal{M}_1(D_{R_1}),\dots,\mathcal{M}_k(D_{R_k}))$ satisfies
$\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}'}$-PrDP with
$\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}'}(r) = \max\{\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}_i}(r)\}_{i=1}^k$. \eop
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A record $r$ lives in exactly one slice, so changing $r$ perturbs exactly one component,
whose mechanism carries its own $\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}_i}(r)$ bound; the other
components are identical functions of unchanged data.
\end{proof}

The sequential twin states
$\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}'}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^k \varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}_i}(r)$ (Lemma
of the paper): sums along chains, maxima across slices --- the same two rules as
Theorems~\ref{thm:dp-seq} and~\ref{thm:dp-parallel}, now pointwise in the record. The
sum mechanism calibrates each slice's Laplace scale to the largest sensitivity that
slice contributes; because slice budgets double, the per-slice noise forms a geometric
series on the order of its largest term --- error governed by the best-budgeted
survivors, not by $\varepsilon_{\min}$. An LDP variant (Theorem of the paper) removes
$O\bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt n}{\varepsilon_{\min}(D)}\log\log(\cdot/\beta)\bigr)$ records
instead, the $\sqrt n$ inflation being the standard price of aggregating $n$ locally
randomized responses. Near-optimality is certified against the \emph{down-neighborhood
lower bound}
$L_\varepsilon(D) = \max_{M' \in \mathcal{M}} \min_{D'\,:\, d(D,D') \le
\varepsilon^{-1}} \{\tau : \Pr[|M'(D') - Q(D')| \le \tau] \ge \tfrac23\}$
(Definition of the paper, quoting prior work): no mechanism beats the error of the best
mechanism on the best nearby dataset, and the paper's mechanisms sit within a constant
factor $c$ of this bound ($(\varepsilon,c)$-down-neighborhood optimality).

\paragraph{What it buys.}
Regulated deployments can honor per-record protection levels --- different $\varepsilon$
per customer or record class --- without giving up the machinery of standard DP: any
existing mechanism drops into the framework, the error adapts to the budgets actually
present in the data, and the optimality certificate says the residual error is the
unavoidable price of the worst-protected records, not an artifact of the design.

\section{Advanced usage and further reading}
\label{sec:dp-advanced}

Everything so far composes \emph{statistics}. Two harder corpus uses push the same
calculus onto objects one would not at first call releases --- memory access patterns
and distance labels; each is sketched in one paragraph, and the chapter's deferred
proofs live in the Appendix (Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}).

\paragraph{Differentially oblivious multi-way join \citep{sigmod26-073}.}
Running a join in untrusted memory exposes not the output but the \emph{access pattern}
--- which cells were read, and when. Fully oblivious (FO) algorithms erase that leak at
the price of touching the whole memory; the paper defines \emph{differentially
oblivious} (DO) algorithms, whose access-pattern distribution satisfies DP over
neighboring input instances, and asks how cheap a DO multi-way join can be. The meta
algorithm is the ledger of Figure~\ref{fig:dp-ledger} with an access pattern as the
composed object: an $(\varepsilon_1,\delta_1)$-DO subroutine releases an
$(\varepsilon_2,\delta_2)$-DP \emph{upper bound} $\widehat{OUT}$ on the join size
$|Q(I)|$, calibrated via smooth sensitivity --- the paper proves smoothness lemmas for
the join's residual-set terms (RST/EST monotonicity under dropping attributes, plus a
``relaxed smooth sensitivity'' RRS damping local sensitivity by $e^{-\beta k}$ across
$k$-step neighborhoods), and a hybrid construction picks the cheapest valid bound per
subset of relations. The join itself then runs as an \emph{advised FO} algorithm: an FO
algorithm whose access pattern is a deterministic function of the input size $|I|$ and
the advice $\widehat{OUT}$ only --- so its leak is $|I|$ plus one DP release, and the
construction is $(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2,\ \delta_1+\delta_2)$-DO by sequential
composition (Theorem~3.2 of the paper), with post-processing immunity licensing the
pattern as a private object. The payoff: $O((N^w + |Q(I)| + \mathrm{RRS}(I))\cdot
\log N)$ time under data complexity ($w$ = fractional hypertree width) instead of full
obliviousness.

\paragraph{Privacy-preserving shortest path via 2-hop labeling \citep{sigmod26-214}.}
Two parties hold subgraphs of one weighted graph, connected by public cut edges, and
must answer shortest-path distance queries between their vertices without revealing
the private weights. Each party builds a 2-hop labeling of its subgraph --- every
vertex stores exact distances to higher-ranked \emph{boundary} vertices --- and the
parties exchange secret-shared boundary graphs instead of raw data. The proof that
this loses no information is an \emph{induction on the optimal substructure of
shortest paths} (Lemma~4.4 and Lemma~5.2 of the paper): any shortest path crossing
partitions decomposes at boundary vertices, so the combined boundary graph preserves
all boundary-to-boundary distances exactly. Privacy is the truncated Laplace
mechanism: edge weights are perturbed with Laplace noise truncated to a bounded range
--- guaranteeing that the iterative label construction converges (no unbounded noise
ever enters a distance) --- and the released labeling satisfies
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP (Theorem~5.4 of the paper), with weight-neighborhood
$\|w - w'\|_1 \le 1$. The composition lesson: the labeling is built by a pipeline of
per-vertex noisy aggregations composed sequentially, and correctness is proved
independently of privacy --- post-processing immunity lets the exact-labeling argument
ride on top of the noisy weights for free.

\subsection*{Further reading}

\begin{itemize}
\item The monograph: Dwork and Roth \citep{dp-composition-bg3} --- the algorithmic
  foundations, with all composition proofs and the privacy-loss calculus done from
  first principles; the natural companion to this chapter.
\item The originals: Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, and Smith \citep{dp-composition-bg1} ---
  sensitivity and calibrated noise; Dwork, Rothblum, and Vadhan
  \citep{dp-composition-bg2} --- the advanced-composition theorem and the
  privacy-loss random variable.
\item Beyond scalar budgets: Mironov's R\'enyi DP \citep{dp-composition-bg4} composes in
  moments and converts back to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$; Dong, Roth, and Su's Gaussian/f-DP
  \citep{dp-composition-bg5} makes the divergence curve itself the composed object ---
  the direction in which budget accounting is moving.
\item Subsampling: Balle, Barthe, and Gaboardi \citep{dp-composition-bg6} --- privacy
  amplification, the discount rule when mechanisms run on samples.
\item For a refresher with the corpus exemplars in one place: the DP sections of the
  concentration chapter (Section~\ref{sec:conc-ldp} in Chapter~\ref{ch:conc}) treat the
  noise tails; the present chapter treats the accounting.
\end{itemize}

\begin{recap}
\item \textbf{What the tool is.} The DP composition calculus turns per-release
  guarantees into one end-to-end contract: budgets add along adaptive chains
  (sequential), collapse to maxima on disjoint data slices (parallel), survive any
  downstream computation unchanged (post-processing), and inflate linearly in group size
  (group privacy); single releases are calibrated by sensitivity --- Laplace for
  numbers, the exponential mechanism for selections.
\item \textbf{Which rung when.} Same data, several releases: sequential. Disjoint
  records/clusters/slices: parallel. Converting neighborhoods (node$\to$edge,
  group$\to$individual): group privacy. Anything after a private output: free. Many
  releases ($k \gtrsim 50$): advanced or moment accounting.
\item \textbf{Why it works.} Indistinguishability multiplies: a product of
  per-release density ratios bounded by $e^{\varepsilon_i}$ is bounded by
  $e^{\sum_i \varepsilon_i}$; disjointness kills all but one factor; post-processing
  integrates against a fixed output law; distance-$\lambda$ chains telescope.
\item \textbf{What the corpus showed.} Cluster explanations (Section~\ref{sec:dp-298}):
  a three-dial ledger with sequential + parallel + post-processing stages and
  sensitivity-$1$ scores by $\ell_1$ algebra. Sparse histograms
  (Section~\ref{sec:dp-178}): four-case dominance over neighboring supports composed
  with a private upper-bound search, optimal $\ell_\infty$ error. N2E
  (Section~\ref{sec:dp-337}): SVT at logarithmic scale, group privacy at
  $\varepsilon/(6\Delta^*)$, LP rounding of the deletion query. Per-record DP
  (Section~\ref{sec:dp-263}): composition lifted from scalars to budget functions ---
  pointwise sums and maxima --- with down-neighborhood optimality.
\item \textbf{Advanced usage.} The calculus also composes non-statistical objects:
  access patterns of differentially oblivious joins and noisy 2-hop distance labelings
  (Section~\ref{sec:dp-advanced}); deferred proofs are collected in the Appendix
  (Section~\ref{sec:dp-appendix}).
\end{recap}

\section{Appendix}
\label{sec:dp-appendix}

Proofs omitted from the main text are collected here.

\subsubsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-laplace} (Laplace mechanism)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} If $GS_Q$ is the global sensitivity of $Q$, then
$Q(D) + \operatorname{Lap}(GS_Q/\varepsilon)$ satisfies $\varepsilon$-DP.\eop

\begin{proof}
Let $b = GS_Q/\varepsilon$ and write $p_D$ for the density of
$Q(D) + \operatorname{Lap}(b)$ at a point $y$. For every coordinate $j$,
$|(Q(D) - y_j) - (Q(D') - y_j)| \le |Q(D) - Q(D')| \le GS_Q$, so the density ratio is
\begin{align*}
\frac{p_D(y)}{p_{D'}(y)}
&= \prod_j \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{|Q(D')_j - y_j| - |Q(D)_j - y_j|}{b}\Bigr)\\
&\le \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\|Q(D)-Q(D')\|_1}{b}\Bigr)
&\le e^{GS_Q/b} \;=\; e^{\varepsilon}.
\end{align*}
Integrating over any measurable $S$ gives
$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S]$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-seq} (sequential composition)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is
$(\varepsilon_1,\delta_1)$-DP and $\mathcal{M}_2(\cdot,y)$ is
$(\varepsilon_2,\delta_2)$-DP in its first input for every fixed $y$, then
$\mathcal{M}_3(D) = \mathcal{M}_2(D,\mathcal{M}_1(D))$ is
$(\varepsilon_1{+}\varepsilon_2,\ \delta_1{+}\delta_2)$-DP.\eop

\begin{proof}
For $y$ in the range of $\mathcal{M}_1$ let $p_D(y) = \Pr[\mathcal{M}_1(D) = y]$ and
let $q_D(S \mid y) = \Pr[\mathcal{M}_2(D,y) \in S]$. For any measurable $S$ of the
composed output space,
\[
\Pr[\mathcal{M}_3(D) \in S]
= \int q_D(S \mid y)\, d p_D(y).
\]
Let $T = \{y : p_D(y) \le e^{\varepsilon_1} p_{D'}(y)\}$; by the DP bound on
$\mathcal{M}_1$ (applied to the complement), $\Pr[\mathcal{M}_1(D) \notin T] \le
\delta_1$. Splitting the integral at $T$ and using the two per-mechanism bounds on each
part,
\begin{align*}
\Pr[\mathcal{M}_3(D) \in S]
&\;\le\; e^{\varepsilon_2}\!\int_T q_{D'}(S\mid y)\, d p_D(y) + \delta_2\\
&\;\le\; e^{\varepsilon_2} e^{\varepsilon_1}\!\int q_{D'}(S\mid y)\, d p_{D'}(y)
+ e^{\varepsilon_2}\delta_2 + \delta_1,
\end{align*}
which is at most $e^{\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2}
\Pr[\mathcal{M}_3(D') \in S] + (\delta_1+\delta_2)$ for $\varepsilon_2 \ge 0$.
Iteration over $k$ adaptive stages is this argument unrolled along the chain.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-group} (group privacy)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} An $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP mechanism satisfies
$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G) \in S] \le e^{\lambda\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G') \in S] +
\lambda\delta$ for inputs at neighborhood distance $\lambda$.\eop

\begin{proof}
Induction on $\lambda$; $\lambda = 1$ is DP itself. For the step, fix intermediates
$G = G_0 \sim G_1 \sim \dots \sim G_\lambda = G'$ and apply DP twice:
\[
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G_0) \in S]
\le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G_1) \in S] + \delta
\le e^{2\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(G_2) \in S] + e^{\varepsilon}\delta + \delta.
\]
After $\lambda$ steps the error term is $\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda-1} e^{i\varepsilon}\delta
\le \lambda e^{(\lambda-1)\varepsilon}\delta$; since
$\lambda e^{(\lambda-1)\varepsilon} \ge \lambda$ this is absorbed by
$\lambda\delta$ as claimed (the paper's statement).
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:dp-298-jsd} (JSD sensitivity bound)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} The Jensen--Shannon distance score has sensitivity at
least $\tfrac12$: some neighbors $D \sim D'$ change it by more than $\tfrac12$.\eop

\begin{proof}
Let $D$ have $n \ge 1$ tuples, all carrying value $a$ on attribute $A$, and let
$D_c = \{t\}$ be a singleton cluster. Both $P_A(D)$ and $P_A(D_c)$ are point masses on
$a$, so $d_{JS}(P_A(D), P_A(D_c)) = 0$. Let $D' = D \cup \{t'\}$ and
$D'_c = D_c \cup \{t'\}$ with $t'[A] = a' \ne a$. Then
\[
P_A(D') = \Bigl(\tfrac{n}{n+1}, \tfrac{1}{n+1}\Bigr),
\qquad
P_A(D'_c) = \Bigl(\tfrac12, \tfrac12\Bigr),
\]
over $\{a, a'\}$, and the mixture puts mass
$\tfrac{n}{2(n+1)} + \tfrac14$ on $a$. Writing $H_b$ for the binary entropy (bits),
\[
\mathit{JSD}\bigl(P_A(D'), P_A(D'_c)\bigr)
= H_b\Bigl(\tfrac{n}{2(n+1)} + \tfrac14\Bigr)
- \tfrac12 H_b\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{n+1}\Bigr)
- \tfrac12 H_b\Bigl(\tfrac12\Bigr).
\]
As $n \to \infty$, the mixture mass tends to $\tfrac34$, so the limit is
$H_b(\tfrac34) - 0 - \tfrac12 \cdot 1 = H_b(\tfrac14) - \tfrac12 \approx 0.811 - 0.5
= 0.311$ bits, using $H_b(\tfrac{1}{n+1}) \to H_b(0) = 0$ and $H_b(\tfrac12) = 1$.
Hence for all large $n$ the divergence exceeds $0.30$ bits, so the Jensen--Shannon
\emph{distance} (its square root) exceeds $\sqrt{0.30} > \tfrac12$ while it was $0$ on
$D$ --- a change of more than $\tfrac12$ between neighbors, as computed in the paper.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dp-337-clip} (clipping distance, N2E)}
{\itshape Statement (recalled).} For node-neighboring $G \subseteq G'$ and $k$ = number
of nodes of $G$ of degree $\ge \Delta$, the clipped graphs
$\mathrm{Clip}(G,\Delta)$, $\mathrm{Clip}(G',\Delta)$ differ in at most $\Delta + k$
edges.\eop

\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be the node of $G' \setminus G$. Count each direction of the difference.
\emph{Edges present after clipping $G'$ but not after clipping $G$}: any such edge
$e = (v_1, v_2)$ with both endpoints in $V$ is among the $\Delta$ smallest-order edges
of $v_1, v_2$ in both graphs, so it survives both clippings; hence every edge in this
category touches $u$, and $u$ keeps at most $\Delta$ edges --- at most $\Delta$ edges
total. \emph{Edges present after clipping $G$ but not after clipping $G'$}: such an edge
$e$ is among the $\Delta$ smallest-order edges of its endpoints in $G$, yet is pushed
out in $G'$ only if one of its endpoints is \emph{saturated} in $G$ (degree
$\ge \Delta$) and one of the new edges incident to $u$ displaces it in that endpoint's
smallest-order set. Each saturated node loses at most one edge per new edge of $u$, and
the new edges number at most the number of saturated nodes they touch; since $G$ has at
most $k$ saturated nodes, this category contributes at most $k$ edges. Together,
$\Delta + k$.
\end{proof}
