\chapter{Exchange and Greedy Arguments}
\label{ch:exchange-greedy}

Few tools rose as sharply through our SIGMOD'26 corpus as this one: exchange/greedy
arguments tagged 4 papers in the arXiv-visible cohort and jumped to 17 once the ACM-only
mass arrived---one of the largest risers in the survey. The reason is a uniform paper
shape: prove your selection problem NP-hard by reduction, then buy a polynomial-time
algorithm back with a provable approximation factor. In our tag co-occurrences the tool
pairs with hardness 10 times and with an approximation-ratio property 9 times---the
signature of ``NP-hard, but structured.'' When the structure is submodularity, a covering
constraint, or a metric, the greedy (or local-search) algorithm comes with a ready-made
certificate, and the paper's theory section shrinks to checking the hypothesis.

\section{The Tool: Exchange Arguments and Their Guarantees}

An exchange argument certifies a greedy choice by showing that any solution avoiding it
can be \emph{transformed} into one that makes it, at no loss. In a matroid, independent
sets admit pairwise exchanges (if $S$ is independent and smaller than independent $S'$,
some $x \in S' \setminus S$ keeps $S \cup \{x\}$ independent), so greedy is \emph{exact}
for any linear objective~\cite{exchange-greedy-bg1}. Outside matroids exactness dies, but
three structures keep a quantitative exchange certificate alive.

\begin{definition}[submodularity]
$f : 2^U \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is \emph{submodular} if
$f(S \cup \{x\}) - f(S) \ge f(T \cup \{x\}) - f(T)$ whenever $S \subseteq T$ and
$x \in U \setminus T$; it is \emph{monotone} if $S \subseteq T \Rightarrow f(S) \le f(T)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Monotone submodular, cardinality $k$.} Diminishing returns let greedy's
next pick match the average marginal value of the optimum's remaining elements; $k$
geometric decay steps compound to
$f(S_{\mathrm{gre}}) \ge (1 - 1/e) \cdot \max_{|B| = k} f(B)$, and $1 - 1/e$ is tight
unless $P = NP$~\cite{exchange-greedy-bg2}.
\item \textbf{Submodular without monotonicity.} Randomized greedy variants retain a
$1/2$ guarantee (exactly, via double greedy in the unconstrained case) and degrade
gracefully under cardinality constraints~\cite{exchange-greedy-bg5}.
\item \textbf{Covering constraints (set cover).} To cover a universe at minimum weight,
charge each newly covered element the amortized cost of the set covering it; the
averaging step against an optimal cover yields the harmonic factor
$H(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} 1/i$~\cite{exchange-greedy-bg3}.
\item \textbf{Metric structure.} With triangle-inequality distances, exchanging one
center for another (local search) reassigns points through two triangle inequalities:
single swaps give $(3+\varepsilon)$ for $k$-median, multi-swaps $(2+\varepsilon)$%
~\cite{exchange-greedy-bg4}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Five Views from the Corpus}

\subsection{Monotone Submodular Diversity: Greedy at $1-1/e$}

Selecting the $k$ most \emph{diverse} answers to a fixed conjunctive query, where
diversity is defined through a volume assignment $\Omega$ over a universe $U$ of
possible solutions.

\begin{theorem}[volume-based diversity]
Let $\Omega$ be any volume assignment over a universe $U$ of possible solutions. Then
$\delta_\Omega$ is always monotone and submodular. Since $Q$ is fixed and $Q(D)$ is
computable in polynomial time over $D$, the greedy strategy that starts from
$S = \emptyset$ and in each iteration adds the tuple $t \in Q(D)$ maximizing the
marginal diversity $\delta_\Omega(S \cup \{t\})$ returns a $k$-subset $S$ with
\[
\delta_\Omega(S) \;\ge\; \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{e}\Bigr) \cdot
\max_{B \subseteq Q(D),\, |B| = k} \delta_\Omega(B).
\]
\end{theorem}

The factor is earned by pure Nemhauser--Wolsey--Fisher structure: a measure-algebra
argument shows every volume assignment makes $\delta_\Omega$ monotone and submodular,
and greedy inherits $1-1/e$---which is tight (maximum coverage is hard beyond $1-1/e$
unless $P = NP$). The contrast is instructive: for the paper's distance-based analogues
under Hamming or Euclidean metrics the best known ratios are strictly worse and degrade
with $\varepsilon$ or $k$, while every polynomial-time-computable volume assignment gets
the clean factor~\cite{sigmod26-350}.

\subsection{NP-Hard Representative Sampling: Lazy Greedy at $1-1/e$}

Choosing a representative subset of large-scale vector data under a budget: the paper
first proves its Problem~1 (Representative Sampling) NP-hard, then turns the budgeted
objective into submodular form.

\begin{lemma}[submodularity of the sampling objective]
Let $F$ be the sampling objective, built from per-point gains $g_s$ that are each
nondecreasing and concave. Then $F : 2^U \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is nondecreasing and
submodular.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[greedy guarantee]
Let $B$ be the budget and let $A_{\mathrm{gre}}$ be the size-$B$ output of
(lazy-)greedy. Then
\[
F(A_{\mathrm{gre}}) \;\ge\; \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{e}\Bigr) \max_{|A| \le B} F(A).
\]
\end{theorem}

Concavity of every per-point gain $g_s$ is the whole proof: sums of concave lifts of
coverage are submodular, and the classical bound then prices the greedy output at
$1-1/e$---a one-page certificate for a problem the reduction just declared hopeless
exactly~\cite{sigmod26-031}.

\subsection{Greedy Risk Coverage: A Set-Cover-Sized Coreset}

Robust query optimization: given a workload distribution $W$ and plan space $\Pi$,
select a small set of plans whose risk \emph{covers} the workload, forming a coreset
that supports local plan inference.

\begin{lemma}[greedy termination]
Assuming the concentration conditions of the sampling lemmas hold in every round, the
greedy cover terminates with $k_r \le 2\kappa^{*}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[risk coreset via greedy cover]
Let $W$ be a workload distribution. Given plans $\Pi$ with $|\Pi| = N$ and parameters
$\varepsilon \in (0,1]$, $\tau \ge 1$, $\delta \in (0,1)$, let $\kappa^{*}$ denote the
size of the smallest $(\varepsilon, \tau, \delta|W|)$-coreset for risk. Fix any
constants $c_1, c_2 > 1$. There exists an algorithm that samples
$O\bigl((\kappa^{*}/\delta^{2}) \ln N \ln(\delta^{-1})\bigr)$ queries from $W$ and
computes, in time $O\bigl((\kappa^{*}/(\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{2})) \cdot N \ln^{3} N
\ln(\delta^{-1})\bigr)$, a set $C \subseteq \Pi$ of size $O(\kappa^{*} \ln
\delta^{-1})$ such that, with high probability, $C$ is a
$(c_{1}\varepsilon, \tau, c_{2}\delta|W|)$-coreset for risk.
\end{theorem}

This is the set-cover pattern in the wild: risk coverage is submodular, each greedy
round shrinks the residual failure probability geometrically (the termination lemma
caps rounds at twice the optimum), and the price of coverage is the familiar
logarithmic slack $\ln(\delta^{-1})$ in the coreset size. Hoeffding and union bounds
merely ensure the greedy cover runs on faithful proxy risks rather than true
ones~\cite{sigmod26-053}\pods.

\subsection{Kruskal Edge-Exchange: Exact Indexing for Temporal Graphs}

Answering cohesive-subgraph queries (connected components, $k$-core) over \emph{all}
time windows of a temporal graph, from one spanning-forest index built Kruskal-style
with amortized union-find.

\begin{lemma}[affiliated-forest invariant]
Given a spanning edge $e = (u, v, t_1, t_2)$, let $\tau(e)$ be the timestamp such that
$e \in F_{t_1,\tau(e)}$ and $e \notin F_{t_1,\tau(e)+1}$. An affiliated forest
$(V, F_{x,y})$ built by $\mathsf{BuildAF}$ contains $e$ if and only if
$0 \le x \le t_1$ and $t_2 \le y \le \tau(e)$.
\end{lemma}

Here the exchange is exact, not approximate: graphic-matroid cycles let the
construction swap an arriving edge for an expiring one while keeping a forest valid
for every window simultaneously---Lemma~above is the invariant those exchanges
preserve. The certificate is completeness, not a factor: an $O(m \log m)$ construction
and $O(m)$-space index answer any window exactly, with queries in
$O(n + \log^{2} m)$~\cite{sigmod26-200}.

\subsection{Swap Exchanges for Symmetry Breaking in Exact Search}

Exact branch-and-bound for maximum common induced subgraph (MCIS), NP-hard, with
branches pruned using vertex and value symmetries.

\begin{theorem}[soundness of symmetry breaking]
Variable symmetry breaking does not violate the correctness or completeness of the
MCIS solution.
\end{theorem}

The proof is a pure swap: exchanging the conflicting pairs $(v_1,u_2),(v_2,u_1)$ for
$(v_1,u_1),(v_2,u_2)$ maps any pruned branch to a symmetric one considered
\emph{earlier} in the fixed branching order, so some optimal mapping always survives
the pruning. The exchange partner is supplied by automorphisms---two vertices are
modular-symmetric exactly when their transposition is an automorphism of the
graph---turning group symmetry into a completeness certificate for exact
search~\cite{sigmod26-009}.

\begin{reachbox}{When to reach for exchange--greedy}
\begin{itemize}
\item Your selection, covering, or clustering problem is proved NP-hard by reduction,
and the objective has \emph{checkable} structure: diminishing returns
(submodularity), a covering constraint, metric distances, or matroid independence.
\item You want the algorithm's quality to be a theorem, not a benchmark: the exchange
proof is usually a page, and the factor ($1-1/e$, $H(n)$, $3+\varepsilon$, $1/2$, or
exact) is inherited, not re-derived.
\item Audit the hypotheses, not the algorithm: pairwise marginal tests or a
concave-gain decomposition for submodularity; the triangle inequality for metrics; the
exchange axiom for matroids. Near-modular or noisy objectives void the certificate.
\item $1-1/e$ needs \emph{monotone} submodular; without monotonicity fall back to
randomized variants ($1/2$ unconstrained).
\item The same idea certifies \emph{exactness}: forest exchanges and symmetry swaps
prove that pruning or one-pass construction loses nothing---completeness with no
approximation factor at all.
\end{itemize}
\end{reachbox}
